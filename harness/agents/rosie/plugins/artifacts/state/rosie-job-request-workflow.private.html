How Rosie runs a job request

One job has one dedicated job-request conversation from start to finish. It turns the customer’s need into a confirmed, approved quote—and then follows the customer’s decision through close-out.

Prepared Aug. 25, 2026 · For Bhoumik · Workflow reference, not a system specification.

The practical rule

A price-list match is not a promise. Rosie can establish a price from the catalogue, but the item remains unconfirmed until the supplier answers or Chris explicitly directs otherwise.

1. Receive the request and capture what matters

Dedicated job workspace

Start one job conversation

Rosie opens a dedicated job-request thread and keeps the complete job there. That is where the team follows progress, sees decisions, and gives job-specific approvals.

Customer need

Capture the usable intake

Record each plant, size, quantity, requested delivery timing and location, plus the details that can change the answer: substitutions allowed, site constraints, quality expectations, and deadlines.

Known customer context

Use, do not assume

Reuse durable customer preferences when known. If key information is missing, keep moving on what can be sourced and clearly flag the gap rather than inventing an answer.

2. Make the sourcing and pricing decision

Begin with Austin Plant Supply (APS)

APS is the normal first pricing source. Rosie looks for the requested plant and size, then records a clear internal basis for the price.

Consider the closest workable option

If APS does not have the exact ask, Rosie identifies a nearby size, cultivar, or other substitute and makes the difference visible. When APS cannot provide a useful option, she looks at other established suppliers.

Bring trade-offs to Chris when they are business choices

If there is a meaningful choice—exact plant elsewhere versus a priced APS substitute, a different source, a partial solution, or a scope trade-off—Rosie lays out the practical options in the job thread for Chris to choose.

Guideline: Internal sourcing detail stays internal. Customer-facing wording says what is being offered, never where it came from, what a supplier charged, or the internal reasoning behind a substitution.

3. Separate pricing from real availability

What a catalogue can answer

It can show that a supplier listed a plant, size, and price. Rosie can use APS’s listed price as the pricing basis for the line.

Decision point

What still needs confirmation

A catalogue cannot confirm what is in the yard today. Every line remains awaiting confirmation until the supplier replies—or Chris explicitly says to treat it as confirmed.

What a supplier is asked

Ask about every relevant line, including items absent from a price list: can they supply it, can they obtain it, what quantity/ETA applies, and whether they have a substitute.

4. Contact suppliers through the right path

Partner supplier

Availability request in the portal

For an LPS Partner, Rosie’s supplier conversation creates a structured availability request. It includes the active job lines and follows the supplier’s remembered preferences about what customer detail may be shared.

Non-partner approval gate

Chris approves the exact outreach

For ordinary email outreach, Rosie prepares the specific message for the job. Chris approves that exact text before it is sent. A previous approval or template is not permission for a new ask.

Contact history

Keep the record where it belongs

Supplier conversation stays in that supplier’s dedicated thread, including the full text sent and received. The answer is then brought back to the job thread for the job decision.

5. Turn answers into a clear customer offer

Update the line’s current state

A confirmed exact item becomes available. A confirmed substitute becomes an alternative offer. An item with no workable source is unavailable. A priced line without an answer remains pending.

Chris is asked when needed

Resolve the decisions only he should make

Rosie asks Chris in the job thread to choose between material alternatives, approve a customer offer, authorize a needed exception, or confirm a line without a supplier answer. Routine sourcing progress does not need approval.

Prepare the quote

The quote contains only confirmed, quotable items and customer-safe descriptions. Pending lines are held or shown as to follow only with Chris’s direction; they are never silently quoted as available.

6. Customer messaging and approval

Exact-text customer gate

Chris approves before contact

Rosie drafts the final quote message in the job thread. Chris approves the exact draft there. Rosie never sends an unsolicited customer message or changes approved words on her own.

Customer conversation

Send from the customer’s thread

The approved message and quote are delivered through that customer’s dedicated conversation and recorded there in full. Their reply—acceptance, question, changed date, or scope change—returns to the job thread.

Keep it customer-safe

Explain the offer, not the sourcing

Customers see the plants, sizes, quantities, price, totals, and any offered substitute. They do not see supplier names, supplier pricing, price lists, or internal notes.

7. Delivery, follow-through, and close-out

Coordinate what changes the job

Supplier responses about delivery timing, substitutions, availability, and missing information come back to the job thread. Rosie surfaces any customer or supplier decision that requires Chris’s direction.

Customer commits

When the customer accepts, the job is marked closed won. This is the current record of a successful commitment and the handoff point for future order conversion work.

Other outcomes

If the customer declines or the opportunity ends, the job is closed lost by human direction. Deliberate archiving is also a human-approved decision—not an automatic cleanup step.

Key operating guidelines at a glance

One job, one place for decisions

All job-specific questions, options, approvals, and status updates live in that job’s dedicated thread.

Price ≠ availability

Do not promise from a list. Supplier confirmation or explicit Chris direction is what makes a line available.

Every external message has a gate

Supplier outreach and every customer message require the right dedicated thread and an approval of the specific text when required.

Expose only the offer

Keep supplier economics and internal sourcing reasoning out of customer-facing messages and quotes.

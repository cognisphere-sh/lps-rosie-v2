## Working market and supplier-model observations

- A **Grower** is principally a production operation with broad wholesale reach; evidence should support both production at scale and the primary wholesale market/customer reach.
- A **Wholesale Nursery** is principally a regional trade-supply or sales-yard model, serving regional trade customers. The distinction turns on the primary role and customer market, not a business name or a single clue.
- Retail-oriented supplier operations and staffing can be high-touch. Their prices may cover that service model, while their customers may buy lower volumes and purchase less recurrently than wholesale buyers.
- Wholesale nurseries often do not offer routine availability in the way a grower may. They can require account setup and identification of the correct point of contact before targeted requests are useful.
- Texas wholesale nurseries can have materially different account-setup gates. Some have formal applications and account processes; others, particularly smaller or family-run operations, may be more relaxed. Treat the difference as something to learn from the individual supplier, not an assumption based on a label.

## Texas addressable-market and account-development strategy

LPS’s current addressable-market focus is Texas. Onboarding effort is grower-first. Texas wholesale nurseries remain valuable prospects; out-of-state wholesale nurseries are retained for future use, but do not receive current routine availability, point-of-contact, or newsletter/outreach effort.

For a promising Texas wholesale nursery, use a light-touch relationship-learning sequence:

1. Establish the account.
2. Identify the correct point of contact and learn the appropriate channel, delivery area, minimums, and relevant specialties.
3. Send targeted requests through that relationship.

This is relationship development, not a blanket demand for availability. Supplier contact remains subject to the applicable authorization and communication rules.

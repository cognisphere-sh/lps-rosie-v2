# Keep a reviewed playbook for writing in Chris’s voice

- Status: drafted
- Route: bhoumik
- Raised: 2026-08-28 by Chris (via Rosie/Lily discussion)
- Updated: 2026-08-28

## Problem

Rosie has general writing guidance and can remember a supplier’s own preferences, but LPS has no durable, reviewed source for Chris’s voice across recurring messages. Individual guidance and examples can drift, be hard to find, or be used without knowing whether Chris still endorses them.

## Evidence

- 2026-08-28 — After correcting the tone of supplier availability outreach, Chris asked what tools would let LPS “capture his voice and communicate authentically like him”—whether that means only guidance or something more.
- Current support: Rosie’s skills contain general drafting rules and `suppliers.notes` carries per-supplier preferences. Neither is a dedicated, Chris-reviewed voice profile or reusable example bank.

## What it should do

Provide a durable LPS voice playbook that agents can consult while drafting messages. It should combine concise writing principles with approved examples for defined message situations and audiences, such as a non-Partner supplier availability ask.

Each example and rule must have an explicit review state so agents can use only material Chris has approved. When Chris edits and approves a real draft, that final text may be captured as a **candidate** example for later review. It must not silently become permanent guidance: only recurring, intentional patterns enter the approved playbook. An approved example guides a new draft; it does not authorize sending it or replace the existing per-message Chris approval gate. Retired or superseded examples remain traceable but are not used for new drafts.

## Acceptance criteria

- A reusable playbook can hold voice principles and examples with their intended audience/situation.
- An agent can distinguish approved, draft, and retired material before using it to shape a draft.
- Each approved entry identifies Chris’s review/approval and when it was last reviewed.
- Chris-edited, approved real messages can be captured as reviewable candidate examples, without automatically becoming approved guidance.
- A draft may use an approved example as guidance but still requires the existing exact-draft approval before external send.
- Supplier-specific communication preferences continue to override the general playbook.

## Out of scope

- Automatic external sending or blanket permission to send templated messages.
- Mimicking Chris’s voice for unreviewed contexts or inventing personal details.
- Replacing supplier/customer-specific preferences.

## Assumptions

- The initial playbook can begin with the approved supplier-availability guidance and a small number of examples rather than attempting to model every kind of LPS communication at once.
- A recurring intentional pattern is a candidate for the playbook; an isolated approved message remains a one-off unless Chris elects to preserve it.

## Open questions

- Which first message situations should be covered: supplier availability asks only, or customer and follow-up messages too?
- Is Chris the only reviewer/approver, or may a named LPS reviewer prepare entries that Chris later approves?

Supplier orders

LPS creates a supplier order only after the customer has committed to the plants and quantities. The order then moves through its own three steps: New, then Processing once the supplier acknowledges it and gives a fulfillment date, then Order Ready. The customer's job stays at Committed for that whole time.

BusinessSupplier partnersTechnical detail collapsed~14 min read

In short

A supplier order is LPS's commitment to buy plants from one supplier. Usually that supplier is Austin Plant Supply (APS), unless Chris picks another one for the job. The main rules:

  • No customer commitment, no order. An order covers only the lines and quantities the customer agreed to. Chris passing on the customer's yes counts. A supplier's answer never counts as the customer accepting.
  • Creating the order commits the job. Every job in the order moves from Quote or Availability to Committed. The order itself starts at New.
  • An order can cover several jobs, and one line can be split. One order can combine demand from several customer jobs for the same supplier. One supplier answer can also be split between separate orders. The app tracks how many units belong to each job. That record is called an allocation.
  • The supplier commits to a date. When the supplier acknowledges the order, they enter a fulfillment date and can add a note. The order then moves to Processing. When the plants are ready, it moves to Order Ready.
  • Pickup or delivery is Chris's decision. Only Chris (the admin) sets whether an order is picked up or delivered. Suppliers can confirm and mark an order ready while it is still unset.
  • Rosie hears about supplier changes. When a supplier confirms an order, marks it ready, changes the date or note, or saves plant status changes, the app sends Rosie (the sales and orders agent) a short notice on that supplier's chat thread.
Where this fits

Orders are the fourth step of the sales flow (Quote → Availability → Committed → Won/Lost). The availability step is optional. A customer who commits straight from the quote can be ordered directly. For the whole flow, see Quote to order, end to end.

When an order is created

Chris creates orders from a job's Orders tab. Rosie creates them with the same database functions once the customer has committed. There are two ways to build one. The app picks the right one automatically:

PathWhen it is usedWhat the order lines copy
From supplier answers (availability-backed)The chosen supplier has answered lines on an open availability request (AR) for this job. This is the usual path after the availability step.The supplier's answer: plant name, size, price and answer status. The note for the supplier combines LPS's ask and the supplier's reply. The note for the customer comes from the job line.
Direct order (from the quote)The chosen supplier has no answered AR lines for this job, but quoted job lines still have quantity left to order. This covers committed intake ("order these") and accepted quotes.The job line itself: name, size, price, status and note for the customer; no AR is involved. Each line keeps the job line's own status, and its note for the supplier starts empty.

The usual workflows:

  • Normal job: quote → availability request → the customer commits → an order built from the supplier's answers.
  • Committed intake: the customer's first message is already an order. Rosie creates the job and its lines, keeps any prices the customer gave, fills missing prices from the APS price list, and creates a direct order without a quote or an AR.
  • Accepted quote: the customer accepts an existing quote → a direct order, with no AR.
Rules Rosie follows

Rosie creates an order only when the customer has committed. She never orders the same demand twice; if an existing order already covers the lines, she reuses it. She never creates, answers or submits an AR just so an order becomes possible. Every ordered line needs a known positive quantity and a price. She never copies the customer's expected date (EDD) into the supplier's fulfillment date.

How the two paths are chosen and stored

The job Orders tab loads GET /api/orders?job=<id>. That call returns two lists. candidates holds answered AR lines that can still be ordered (orderCandidates). directCandidates holds quoted job lines with demand left (directOrderCandidates). The form uses the direct path only when the selected supplier has no AR-backed candidates but direct candidates exist (useDirect in components/workspace/orders.tsx).

  • The AR-backed path calls the database function create_order(...). Each item names a candidate_id (sent by the app as availabilityRequestItemId) plus its allocations.
  • The direct path calls create_direct_order(...). Its items are {job_request_item_id, quantity}, with one allocation per line.
  • Since migration 0112, order lines keep no pointer back to the AR. The candidate id is used only to validate and copy the answer at creation time. The "related availability requests" shown on an order are worked out on each read. They come from the order's job lines and include only ARs of the order's own supplier, so they are not a stored record of where the order came from.
  • On the AR path, a can_order answer is copied onto the order line as need_to_order, which the order screens label Need to Order.
  • Both functions move every allocated job that is in quoted or availability to committed. In the app, "Committed is set by order creation" is a convention Rosie follows. The job status picker does not block setting it by hand.

Known risk, inferred from the source and not seen live: a direct order of a job line whose status is Diff Sz/Sub (stored as alternative_proposed) copies that value. The order-line status check does not allow it, so the creation would probably fail with a 400.

The order lifecycle

An order has its own status, separate from the job's. While the order moves forward, the job stays Committed, because jobs have no "Confirmed" stage.

StatusStored valueColourMeaningHow it is reached
NewnewBlueCreated and waiting for the supplier to acknowledge it; it has no fulfillment date yet.Order creation. The app shows "Order created. Its status is New, awaiting partner confirmation."
ProcessingprocessingOrangeThe supplier has acknowledged the order and committed to a fulfillment date.Acknowledge Received dialog: a fulfillment date (required) and an optional note. Records the confirmation date.
Order Readyorder_readyGreenThe plants are ready for pickup or delivery. The supplier portal's filter calls this Ready.Mark as Ready, which needs a fulfillment date to be set.
Closed Wonclosed_wonGreenFinished and won. Counts in closure accounting.Closed by Rosie on Chris's word (see Closing orders).
Closed Lostclosed_lostRedFinished and lost. Its quantities go back to the job lines and can be ordered again.Same as above.
  • Forward only. New → Processing → Order Ready. You cannot skip Processing or go backwards.
  • The fulfillment date is required from Processing onwards, and it cannot be cleared after confirmation. It can still be changed to another date.
  • Plant line statuses are separate. Changing the order's status does not change any line's procurement status (for example, Need to Order).
  • A closed order's outcome is final. Closing it again with the same outcome does nothing. Closing it with the other outcome is refused.
Older wording you may see

Older notes call the actions "Confirm order" and "Mark Order Ready", and an older stage "Confirmed". In the current app the buttons read Acknowledge Received and Mark as Ready, and the stages are New, Processing and Order Ready. Migration 0094 moved old Committed orders to New and old Confirmed orders to Processing.

Where the lifecycle is enforced
  • Vocabulary: ORDER_STATUSES and ORDER_STATUS_META (labels) in app/lib/orders.ts. The database CHECK allows new, processing, order_ready, closed_won, closed_lost. The status pill colours come from orderPalettes in components/workspace/orders.tsx, not from the tone field in ORDER_STATUS_META.
  • Every header change goes through one database function, update_partner_order(p_order_id, p_supplier_id, p_patch). It accepts the keys status, fulfillment_method, fulfillment_date and note_for_supplier. Any other key raises "Unsupported order update". The function locks the linked jobs and then the order. The order must belong to that supplier, be active, and have an active supplier. It refuses closed orders and orders whose linked jobs are all closed ("Closed orders cannot be changed"). Any transition other than New → Processing → Order Ready raises "Orders move from New to Processing to Order Ready". Processing and Order Ready without a date raise "Choose a fulfillment date before confirming the order". The confirmation date is stamped as coalesce(existing, CURRENT_DATE).
  • The app parser parsePartnerOrderUpdate accepts only status: 'processing' | 'order_ready' ("Choose Processing or Order Ready"). It checks that fulfillmentDate is a real calendar YYYY-MM-DD date and that the note is at most 10,000 characters. A blank note clears it; leaving the note out keeps it unchanged.
  • confirm_order(p_order_id, p_supplier_id, p_confirmation_date, p_fulfillment_date) is an older, service-only function that Rosie uses to record a confirmation given by chat or email. It goes through update_partner_order and follows the same rules, then writes the given confirmation date (today by default).

Creating an order

Chris creates orders from the job's Orders tab. The focused job page shows the Request items tab, then Orders, then one tab for each supplier AR. See Jobs and job lines.

  1. Open the form. On the Orders tab, choose Create order. The "Create supplier order" form opens with the current job (JR-<id>) already included.
  2. Add other jobs if needed. Use Add another job… to bring in other open jobs, so that one supplier order covers several customers' demand. Each added job can be removed again.
  3. Pick the supplier. The supplier list defaults to APS when APS has answered lines, otherwise to the first supplier with answered lines. If no supplier has answered lines, it lists all active suppliers, with APS first choice.
  4. Set Pickup / delivery (optional). This defaults to the job's own Pickup/Delivery setting. "To be arranged" leaves it unset.
  5. Tick lines and enter quantities. Each answered AR line is listed with its AR, answer, quantity remaining and price ("Price pending" if there isn't one). Under it is one checkbox and one quantity box for each job it can serve. Ticking a box fills in the most that can still be ordered. For a direct order, each quoted line has an "Order quoted line" checkbox and a quantity.
  6. Add notes (optional). Note for supplier is what the supplier sees. Note for customer is customer-facing and is never sent to the supplier.
  7. Confirm the customer's commitment. Tick "Customer has confirmed the selected quantities for every included job." This box clears whenever the selection changes, so it always applies to exactly what is being ordered.
  8. Create new order. The order is created at New, and every included job moves to Committed.
No date at creation

The creation form has no date picker on purpose. The fulfillment date is the supplier's commitment, so the supplier (or Chris for them) enters it when acknowledging the order. The customer's EDD stays on the job and is shown next to the order for reference.

What the server rejects

Creation fails if the customer-confirmation flag is missing ("Confirm the customer has placed this order"), if a fulfillment date is sent ("The supplier enters the fulfillment date when confirming the order"), if no lines or more than 500 lines are selected, or if any quantity is too large for what is left (see the next section).

Create-order request and validation

POST /api/orders (admin only). Rosie calls the same database functions directly. Body for an order built from supplier answers:

{
  "supplierId": 1,
  "customerConfirmed": true,
  "fulfillmentMethod": "pickup" | "delivery" | null,
  "noteForSupplier": "…" | null,
  "noteForCustomer": "…" | null,
  "items": [
    { "availabilityRequestItemId": 42,
      "allocations": [ { "jobRequestItemId": 7, "quantity": 10 } ] }
  ]
}

For a direct order, each item is { "jobRequestItemId": 7, "quantity": 10 }. The request counts as direct when every item has jobRequestItemId and none has availabilityRequestItemId. The response is 201 { id }.

  • parseCreateOrder (app/lib/orders.ts) requires a positive integer supplierId and customerConfirmed === true. It rejects a non-empty fulfillmentDate and checks that fulfillmentMethod is pickup, delivery or null. It also rejects the old keys notes ("Use noteForSupplier instead of notes") and noteFromCustomer ("Use noteForCustomer instead of noteFromCustomer"). Notes are limited to 10,000 characters.
  • An order has 1–500 items, and each item has 1–500 allocations. IDs must be unique within their list. Quantities must be positive, finite numbers up to 109.
  • The app always sends p_fulfillment_date = null to the database. The database functions do accept a date. Rosie's rules allow her to pass one only when it is actually known, and never the customer's EDD.
  • Database errors are returned as 400 { error }. Non-admins get 401 or 403.

Quantities and allocations across jobs

An order is placed with one supplier, but customer demand belongs to jobs. LPS links the two with allocations. Each order line records how many of its units belong to which job line. This allows:

  • Combining jobs. One APS order can carry 10 units for one customer's job and 5 for another's, as a single line of 15.
  • Splitting a line. Part of a supplier's answer can go into one order now, and the rest into another order later.
  • Using several suppliers. A job line can be ordered partly from one supplier and partly from another. Every order counts against the same customer demand, whichever supplier it is with and whether it is direct or built from answers.

To prevent over-ordering, the app limits every quantity:

LimitWhat it meansError if exceeded
Remaining customer demandThe job line's demanded quantity minus what is already allocated on active orders with any supplier."Order exceeds remaining customer demand"
Remaining requested quantity (AR path)What was asked of this supplier for that line, minus what is already ordered from this supplier for it."Order exceeds remaining requested quantity"
Supplier's offer (AR path)No more than the quantity the supplier said is available. Can Order is the exception: the supplier will source the stock, so having none on hand is not a limit."Order exceeds supplier available quantity"
Supplier answer on the ARCan it be ordered?Status on the order line
AvailableYesAvailable
Partial QtyYes, up to the quantity offeredPartial Qty
Can OrderYes, with no stock limitNeed to Order
Arriving SoonYesArriving Soon
Diff Sz/Sub (the supplier offers a substitute)The asked plant is stored as Unavailable and cannot be ordered. The substitute becomes its own line on the same AR, with the offer's price and quantity, and that line can be ordered.Available, or Partial Qty if the offered quantity is short (the substitute)
Pending (no answer yet)No—
UnavailableNo—
Lost orders release quantity

An order closed as Lost, or no longer active, stops counting against demand. Those units can then be ordered again, from the same supplier or another one. Orders that are open or closed as Won keep counting.

Never count a shared line twice

A combined order line serves several jobs. Each job's share is its allocation, not the line's total. Rosie's rules say: "A shared total is never each job's amount."

In the Create order form, a quoted line with no demanded quantity (unknown) is not offered for a direct order. Its remaining quantity counts as zero until a quantity is set.

How remaining quantities are calculated and enforced
  • Allocations are stored in order_item_job_request_items(order_item_id, job_request_item_id, quantity > 0). Both foreign keys cascade on delete. Orders have no single job foreign key; an order's jobs are found through its allocations.
  • "Already ordered" counts active order lines on active orders whose status is not closed_lost.
  • orderCandidates(jobIds) works out each answered candidate's capacity as min(candidate demand, offered). The offer counts as unlimited for can_order or when no available quantity was given. It then subtracts what is already ordered from this supplier for that job line. Each job's quantity box is also limited by that job line's remaining customer demand.
  • directOrderCandidates(jobIds) lists active lines of open jobs where demanded − ordered > 0.
  • The browser checks the same limits before submitting ("Enter positive quantities within the job and supplier quantities remaining"). The database checks them again inside one transaction. create_order and create_direct_order take a per-supplier advisory lock (order-supplier:<id>) and then lock the jobs and source ARs. This way, two orders created at the same time cannot both use up the same demand. Every job must be active and not closed ("This job request is closed"). Each allocation must be the candidate's own job line ("Job line is not allocated to this availability line"). Each candidate must be answered and not Unavailable ("Supplier must answer availability before ordering").
  • The order line stores a snapshot: name, size, quantity (the sum of its allocations), unit price and status. Later edits to the job or the AR do not change it.

Acknowledging and fulfilling an order

After an order is created, the supplier sees it in the Orders section of the supplier portal. A New order opens with an Acknowledge Received button next to its title.

The Acknowledge Received dialog

FieldRequired?Notes
Customer requestRead-onlyThe customer's expected delivery/installation date (EDD) for each linked job, for reference. When an order combines jobs, the supplier sees each job's date, but not which LPS job it belongs to.
Fulfillment dateYesThe date the supplier commits to. The save button stays disabled until a date is entered.

Saving the dialog moves the order to Processing and records the confirmation date, shown on the order as "Acknowledged: <date>". The date, note and status are saved in one step. The dialog never sets pickup or delivery.

After acknowledgement

  • Edit fulfillment details opens the same dialog as "Fulfillment details". It changes the date or note without changing the status. It is available on Processing and Order Ready orders.
  • Mark as Ready moves a Processing order to Order Ready. It is disabled until a fulfillment date exists.
  • Plant line statuses. On the order detail page, the supplier can change any line's status (for example from Need to Order to Available) and add "Details for Rosie". Changes are staged first. One Save N status changes button then saves them all and "Sends one update to Rosie."
  • Line prices. The supplier can correct a line's unit price inline. The price must be positive with at most two decimals, or blank. Line totals recalculate automatically.
Chris acknowledges from the portal view

On the job's Orders tab, a New order shows no Acknowledge button: the code says confirming an order belongs in the supplier portal. When a supplier confirms by phone or email, Chris can acknowledge for them by opening the supplier's portal view of the order (admins can open any supplier's portal). Rosie can also record the confirmation with confirm_order once the details are clear. Once an order is Processing, the job's Orders tab does show Edit fulfillment details and Mark as Ready.

Not the same as an AR submission

Submitting availability answers never confirms an order. Order acknowledgement is a separate step on the order itself.

Suppliers cannot change pickup or delivery

Any portal request that includes a pickup/delivery value is refused with "Pickup or delivery is managed by Chris" (HTTP 403). This applies to every request through the portal route, including an admin previewing the portal. Chris changes it from the job's Orders tab.

How the admin-only method is enforced
  • PATCH /api/partner/:id/orders/:orderId returns 403 whenever the body contains the key fulfillmentMethod. GET on the same route always returns canEditFulfillmentMethod: false.
  • PATCH /api/orders/:orderId (admin only) accepts fulfillmentMethod: 'pickup' | 'delivery' | null. The admin order card's method pill is an invisible <select> that sends this field.
  • Migration 0098 (applied 2026-09-15) allows Processing and Order Ready with a null method. A fulfillment date is still required. Before that change, the method was needed at confirmation.

What suppliers do see

On an order, the supplier sees the linked job names, the customer contact and delivery address, each job's EDD, the plants, quantities, prices, statuses and the supplier note. They do not see LPS's internal job IDs, the per-job allocations, internal notes or the note for the customer. These are left out of the data the server sends, not just hidden on screen. On availability requests, customer details reach the supplier only when "share customer details" is turned on.

Access checks
  • The /api/orders/** routes check role === 'admin' in each handler. Otherwise they return 401 unauthorized or 403 forbidden.
  • The /api/partner/:id/orders/** routes use requirePartnerAccess. Admins can open any supplier. A partner can open only their own supplier or its company's branches (canAccessSupplier). Any other supplier returns 404 rather than 403, so a partner cannot even confirm that another supplier exists. Each order is looked up with that supplier's scope, so another supplier's order is also a 404.
  • Supplier payloads come from listOrders({ forSupplier: true }). They leave out publicToken, noteForCustomer (on the header and on lines), allocations and shareJobs, and take job IDs and names out of jobSchedules. They do include customerDetails (job name, customer name, company, email, phone and address). Related ARs for suppliers exclude Draft ARs.
  • The header and line PATCH routes (admin and portal) refuse a closed order with 409 "This order is closed". Deleting a line from a closed order returns 404. Publishing a closed order returns 400 with the same message.

Order cards and screens

Chris's order cards (job Orders tab)

Each order is a card that starts collapsed, with its summary and Copy order visible. Each card expands and collapses on its own.

  • First row: the order status pill, the order reference (O-<id>, or a saved record name) and the job name(s). Then the customer's EDD, labelled per job when the order combines jobs. On the right: the Pickup/Delivery pill (which Chris can change), the item count, the subtotal and Copy order.
  • Second row: one pill holding the supplier name (linked to the supplier profile), "Fulfillment: <date>" or "To be confirmed", and the start of the supplier note. Next to it: "Acknowledged: <date>" once confirmed, and links to the related availability requests.
  • Expanded: the action bar (Edit fulfillment details, Mark as Ready, Save status changes), the order-level Note for supplier and Note for customer (shown read-only when set), and the plant lines. Line name, size, quantity, unit price and both notes can be edited inline, and each line has a status selector.

The Orders tab refreshes whenever the job gets a database change event, without losing an order draft that is still being filled in.

Supplier portal

  • Orders queue (/partner/:id/orders): colour-coded New / Processing / Ready filter buttons with counts. These are multi-select. New and Processing are selected on a fresh visit, and there is no "All" button. A floating selector switches between By order, By status (grouped by plant line status) and By plant.
  • Order cards show the status, job name(s), a date pill, the Pickup/Delivery tag, item count, subtotal, the customer company or name, and the supplier note. Tapping a card opens the order page.
  • Order page (/partner/:id/orders/:orderId): the same header as the availability pages (job name, status, method and fulfillment date, customer contacts, supplier note), the related AR links, a summary strip of line counts by status with the subtotal, and the plant rows. The rows show Qty, Size, Name, Status, Unit price, Line total and Note for supplier. Actions are pinned at the bottom. A print sheet lists the order, supplier, status, schedule, supplier note, related ARs and every line with its subtotal.
  • Order/Request History (/partner/:id/closed-orders): closed orders, shown read-only.
  • Portal order screens refresh on the app's live change signal, when the window gets focus, when the tab becomes visible again, when the browser comes back online, on a 60-second fallback timer while visible, and from the Refresh button.
No customer-wide order view yet

The Customers pages do not show order totals. To see a customer's orders, open the Orders tab of each of their jobs.

Components and labels
  • OrderCard renders both views. With a supplierId it shows the portal detail layout. Without one it shows Chris's compact card. PartnerOrderCard is the portal list row. OrderStatusFilters and OrderViewControls drive the queue (components/workspace/partner-orders.tsx, where the default is ['new','processing']).

Copy order and public order links

Copy order

Copy order on Chris's card opens a small menu with two choices:

ChoiceLink includedNotes
PublicThe order's public link (/public/orders/<token>) at the top, and each job heading links to it tooPublishes the order first if it isn't public yet. Not available for closed orders.

The copied content is a formatted table, plus a plain-text version for apps that don't take formatting. In a browser without rich clipboard support, the HTML source is copied instead. It contains the supplier, Pickup or delivery, fulfillment date and order note. For each linked job, listed once, it adds the job name, customer (company and name), email and phone when known, delivery address and installation date (EDD). Then come the plant lines (Quantity, Size, Plant Name, Unit Price, Total, Note for supplier), the subtotal and the item count. Missing values read "Not provided" or "To be confirmed". Customer contact details reach the clipboard only from Chris's card.

Public order links

A public order link lets a supplier without a portal login see one order without signing in. It is read-only.

  • The page shows the order the way a supplier sees it in the portal, labelled "Shared order · Read-only". It shows plants, quantities, prices, statuses, the supplier note, schedules, and the fulfillment contacts and addresses. It refreshes every 30 seconds and when the window gets focus.
  • It leaves out customer notes, allocation IDs, related availability requests and the token itself. The page asks search engines not to index it and sends no referrer.
  • There are no public write actions. Someone with the link cannot acknowledge the order or mark it ready. For a supplier without a login, Chris or Rosie records their confirmation.
  • The link stops working as soon as the order closes, becomes inactive, its supplier is deactivated, or all its linked jobs close. Publishing again creates a new link, and old links stay dead.
Anyone with the link can see it

A public order link shows the same customer contact and delivery details as the supplier portal. Share it only with the supplier it is meant for.

Public link mechanics
  • PATCH /api/orders/:orderId with exactly { "isPublic": true | false } (admin). Publishing writes a fresh random UUID to orders.public_token. Revoking sets it to null. A closed order cannot be published ("This order is closed"). The database trigger guard_order_public_link (migration 0114) clears the token when an order closes or becomes inactive, and refuses to publish for an inactive supplier.
  • GET /api/public/orders/:token accepts only a UUID-shaped token. It requires an active order that is not Closed Won/Lost, an active supplier, and a linked job that is still open. It checks the token again after reading the order, so a link revoked mid-read stays dead. Responses carry Cache-Control: no-store and Referrer-Policy: no-referrer. An invalid link returns 404 "This link is no longer available".
  • The request gate lets /public/orders/<uuid> and /api/public/orders/<uuid> through without a session.
  • The Copy order menu currently creates links but cannot revoke them. A Make private control exists in order-sharing.tsx, but no screen uses it, so revoking is done with the API (isPublic: false) or happens automatically when the order closes.

See Supplier portal and public links.

Notifications and live updates

When a supplier changes an order in the portal, the app sends Rosie a short "wake-up" notice. It lands on that supplier's Rosie thread, or on her main thread if the supplier doesn't have one yet. Rosie then re-reads the order and passes the news to every linked job.

What happenedEventWhat Rosie sees
The supplier acknowledged the order, marked it ready, or changed the fulfillment date or supplier noteorder_updated"<supplier> confirmed | marked ready | updated order O-<id>. Changes: …", with each field's old and new value.
The supplier saved one or more plant line status changesorder_status_saved"<supplier> saved N order status change(s) on order O-<id>.", with each line's status and the supplier's details.
  • A notice is sent only when a value actually changed. The save always happens first. If the notice fails, the supplier's change is still saved, and the failure is only logged.
  • A supplier event never counts as Chris's approval or the customer's acceptance, and it never changes pickup/delivery.
  • LPS → supplier: Rosie has standing permission to tell a supplier about any verified change to a placed order. She adds one dated line to the end of the order's supplier note (for example 09-22: qty 10 → 12; pickup 09-24 → 09-25.), then messages the supplier: in the portal chat for partners, or by email for other suppliers. This is the only note in LPS that keeps a history.
  • New orders do not trigger an app event. The order simply appears in the supplier's portal. Rosie passes the details to the supplier thread. Any email or customer message still needs Chris's approval.
  • Live screens: changes to orders, order lines and allocations trigger the app's live refresh signal. Chris's screens and the supplier portal both re-read their data when it arrives, with a 60-second fallback timer. Public order pages do not get the live signal; they refresh every 30 seconds and when the window gets focus.
Event delivery details
  • notifySupplierEvent (app/lib/notify-supplier-events.ts) posts to the harness endpoint /webhook/rosie/supplier-events/notify with the shared-secret header x-app-secret (env var SUPPLIER_EVENTS_APP_SECRET) and a 5-second timeout. If the variable is not set, nothing is sent.
  • The partner header PATCH compares status, fulfillmentDate and noteForSupplier with the saved order. It sends order_updated with orderChanges[] (1–3 entries). The harness plugin accepts status values of processing or order_ready only.
  • POST /api/partner/:id/orders/:orderId/status-notification with { changes: [{ itemId, status, note }] } (1–500 changes) sends order_status_saved. The portal calls it after saving each line status one at a time.
  • Only portal routes send events. That includes an admin using the portal view, in which case the notice is still worded as "<supplier> confirmed…". Changes Chris makes from the job Orders tab (/api/orders/**), price edits and public-link views send no event. order_status_updated is defined but nothing sends it.
  • Live refresh: the tables orders, order_items and order_item_job_request_items are in JOB_REALTIME_TABLES (app/lib/job-events.ts). The server passes their Supabase Realtime changes on over SSE as data-free {type:"jobs"} signals (/api/chat/events for Chris, /api/partner/:id/events for the portal). The browser waits 200 ms to group a burst into one re-read (useJobUpdates).

See Chat and live updates and Harness plugins.

Closing orders

  • Won / Lost is Chris's call. Rosie closes an order as Closed Won or Closed Lost only on Chris's explicit word. A supplier going quiet does not count as a lost sale, and the app has no button that closes an order.
  • Closing can close the job. When the last open order on a job closes, the job closes automatically only if every line has a known quantity and is fully covered by closed orders. If any of those orders was lost, the job is Lost; otherwise it is Won. If not, Rosie asks Chris. A job lost before any order was placed can be closed directly.
  • Archived orders. An order counts as closed, and moves to the portal's Order/Request History, when its own status is Closed Won/Lost or all its linked jobs are closed or inactive. A shared order stays active while any linked job is open. Closed orders are read-only everywhere.
  • Deleting a job (permanent) also deletes orders used only by that job. Orders shared with other jobs are kept.
close_order

close_order(p_order_id, p_status) is a service-only database function, run by Rosie, with no app route. Closing again with the same outcome does nothing. A different outcome raises "Order is unavailable or already closed with a different outcome". For each linked job, it closes the job only if no active order on that job is still New, Processing or Order Ready, and every active line has a non-null demand that closed active orders fully cover. The derived "closed" flag is calculated in listOrders each time orders are read. It is not stored.

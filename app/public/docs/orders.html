Supplier orders

LPS creates a supplier order only after the customer has committed to the plants and quantities. Each order belongs to one job and one supplier. It moves through its own three steps: New, then Processing once the supplier acknowledges it, then Order Ready. The customer's job stays at Committed for that whole time.

BusinessSupplier partnersTechnical detail collapsed~14 min read

In short

A supplier order is LPS's commitment to buy plants from one supplier. Usually that supplier is Austin Plant Supply (APS), unless Chris picks another one for the job. The main rules:

  • No customer commitment, no order. An order covers only the lines the customer agreed to. Chris passing on the customer's yes counts. A supplier's answer never counts as the customer accepting.
  • Creating the order commits the job. The job moves from Quote or Availability to Committed. The order itself starts at New.
  • One job, one supplier, one row per candidate. An order belongs to one job and one supplier, and a job has at most one open order with each supplier. Each order row is a copy of one job line's selected candidate: the supplier's plant, size, price and answer chosen for that line. Ordering more lines from the same supplier adds them to the open order. There is no combining jobs and no splitting a line.
  • Mode and date come from the job. An order has no pickup/delivery or date of its own. Its Mode is the job's Pickup/Delivery and its Target date is the job's EDD, so Chris changes them on the job. Acknowledging an order takes no input.
  • Additional details tell the supplier how to deliver. Each order carries a short list of fields (customer, delivery address, contacts, payment method) and a Change log of material changes. Only LPS writes them.
  • Rosie hears about supplier changes. When a supplier acknowledges an order, marks it ready, or saves plant status changes in the portal, the app sends Rosie (the sales and orders agent) a short notice on the job's chat thread.
Where this fits

Orders are the fourth step of the sales flow (Quote → Availability → Committed → Won/Lost). The availability step is optional. A customer who commits straight from the quote can be ordered directly from the lines' selected candidates. For the whole flow, see Quote to order, end to end.

When an order is created

Chris creates orders from a job's Orders tab. Rosie creates them with the same database function, create_order, once the customer has committed. There is one way to build an order: Chris (or Rosie) picks a supplier and the job lines to order, and each line's selected candidate becomes one order row.

Order row fieldCopied from
Plant nameThe selected candidate's name.
SizeThe candidate's size, or the job line's size if the candidate has none.
QuantityThe quantity the job line shows available (never more than its Qty), otherwise its Qty.
Unit priceThe candidate's price. A line with no price is ordered unpriced and priced on the order later. $0 is a valid price.
StatusThe candidate's answer. An unanswered candidate becomes Pending, and Can Order becomes Need to Order. Every other answer is copied as it is (see What each order row copies).
Note for supplierLPS's note on the candidate (what LPS asked the supplier).
Note from supplierThe supplier's reply on the candidate.

The usual workflows:

  • Normal job: quote → availability request → the customer commits → an order built from the candidates selected from the supplier's answers.
  • Committed intake: the customer's first message is already an order. Rosie creates the job and its lines, keeps any prices the customer gave, fills missing prices from the APS price list, and orders the lines without an AR. Each line's selected candidate is then its default APS candidate.
  • Accepted quote: the customer accepts an existing quote → an order of the quoted lines' selected candidates, with no AR.
Rules Rosie follows

Rosie creates an order only when the customer has committed, and only for the lines committed to. A line whose selected candidate is already on an order cannot be ordered again. She never creates, answers or submits an AR just so an order becomes possible. A line needs a quantity (an available quantity or a Qty). Unpriced and unanswered lines can be ordered and fixed on the order afterwards. Pickup/delivery and the target date are set on the job, not on the order.

How lines are offered and stored

The job Orders tab loads GET /api/orders?job=<id>, which returns { orders, lines, additionalDetails }. lines (orderLineChoices) lists the job's active lines whose selected candidate is not on an order yet and whose supplier is active. Each comes with the candidate's supplier, name, size, status, quantity and price, as create_order will copy them. additionalDetails (newOrderDetails) is the field list a new order starts from.

  • create_order(p_job_request_id, p_supplier_id, p_job_request_item_ids bigint[], p_additional_details jsonb) copies each line's selected candidate. The status maps NULL → pending and can_order → need_to_order, and keeps every other value. The quantity is coalesce(nullif(least(available, Qty), 0), Qty).
  • order_items.candidate_id links each row to its candidate (0135). It is unique since 0146, so a candidate is on at most one row. Rows added by hand have no candidate.
  • The order's availability request is the supplier's AR for the same job (one per job and supplier). It is worked out on each read and is not stored.
  • create_order moves the job from quoted or availability to committed. In the app, "Committed is set by order creation" is a convention Rosie follows. The job status picker does not block setting it by hand.
  • create_direct_order was dropped in 0146, so there is no separate "direct" path. The old Diff Sz/Sub value alternative_proposed was retired in 0130, and the stored value diff_size_sub is allowed on order rows.

The order lifecycle

An order has its own status, separate from the job's. While the order moves forward, the job stays Committed, because jobs have no "Confirmed" stage.

Order Readyorder_readyGreenThe plants are ready for pickup or delivery. The supplier portal's filter calls this Ready.Mark as Ready, then Confirm: ready for <target date>.

  • Forward only. New → Processing → Order Ready. You cannot skip Processing or go backwards.
  • No date needed. Acknowledging and marking ready need no date. The target date shown is the job's EDD.
  • Plant line statuses are separate. Changing the order's status does not change any row's procurement status (for example, Need to Order).
  • A closed order's outcome is final. Closing it again with the same outcome does nothing. Closing it with the other outcome is refused.
Older wording you may see

Older notes call the actions "Confirm order" and "Mark Order Ready", and an older stage "Confirmed". In the current app the buttons read Acknowledge Received and Mark as Ready, and the stages are New, Processing and Order Ready. Migration 0094 moved old Committed orders to New and old Confirmed orders to Processing. Notes from before 2026-10-21 may also mention a supplier "fulfillment date"; orders no longer have one.

Where the lifecycle is enforced
  • Vocabulary: ORDER_STATUSES and ORDER_STATUS_META (labels and tones) in app/lib/orders.ts. The database CHECK allows new, processing, order_ready, closed_won, closed_lost. The status pill colour comes from orderPalette() in components/workspace/orders.tsx, which reads the tone in ORDER_STATUS_META.
  • Every header change goes through one database function, update_partner_order(p_order_id, p_supplier_id, p_patch). It accepts the keys status, additional_details, change_log and note_from_supplier. Any other key raises "Unsupported order update". The function locks the order's job and then the order. The order must belong to that supplier, be active, and have an active supplier. It refuses closed orders and orders whose job is closed ("Closed orders cannot be changed"). Any transition other than New → Processing → Order Ready raises "Orders move from New to Processing to Order Ready". additional_details must pass is_order_details; the change log and the supplier's note are limited to 10,000 characters. There is no date requirement. On New → Processing the confirmation date is stamped as coalesce(existing, CURRENT_DATE).
  • The app parser parsePartnerOrderUpdate accepts only status, additionalDetails, changeLog and noteFromSupplier ("Choose an order status, additional details, change log, or note"). status must be 'processing' | 'order_ready' ("Choose Processing or Order Ready"). A blank text clears it; leaving a key out keeps it unchanged.
  • confirm_order(p_order_id, p_supplier_id, p_confirmation_date DEFAULT CURRENT_DATE) is an older, service-only function that Rosie uses to record a confirmation given by chat or email. It moves the order to Processing through update_partner_order, follows the same rules, then writes the given confirmation date. Since 0149 it takes no fulfillment date.

Creating an order

Chris creates orders from the job's Orders tab. The focused job page shows the Request items tab, then Orders, then one tab for each supplier AR. See Jobs and job lines.

  1. Open the form. On the Orders tab, choose Create order. The form "Order selected candidates · JR-<id>" opens. If every line's selected candidate is already on an order, it says so.
  2. Pick the supplier. The Supplier list holds the suppliers of the lines' selected candidates. APS is selected by default when it has lines.
  3. Untick what is not being ordered. Every line for that supplier is listed as "qty × size name · status · price" ("No price" when unpriced), and every line starts ticked. Chris unticks the lines the customer has not committed to. There is no quantity box: the quantity comes from the line.
  4. Check the Additional details. "Additional details (shown to the supplier)" starts with Customer, Delivery address and Delivery contact from the job. Chris can edit, reorder, remove or add fields. If the supplier already has an open order on this job, the editor is hidden and the form says "These lines are added to O-<id>, the open order with this supplier."
  5. Review and save. The form shows the total of the ticked rows, how many of them have no price, and that the supplier sees the order (or the added rows) in their portal straight away. The button reads Create order with N rows, or Add N to O-<id>. A new order starts at New, and the job moves to Committed.
No date or method on the order

The form has no date picker and no pickup/delivery choice. The order's Mode and Target date are the job's Pickup/Delivery and EDD, so set them on the job (in Job details), ideally before ordering.

What the server rejects

Creation fails if no lines or more than 500 lines are ticked, if a line's candidate is selected from another supplier or is already on an order, or if a line has no quantity ("Set a quantity for … before ordering"). It also refuses the old pickup/delivery and note fields ("Set pickup/delivery on the job and send additionalDetails").

Create-order request and validation

POST /api/orders (admin only). Rosie calls the same database function directly. Body:

{
  "jobRequestId": 42,
  "supplierId": 1,
  "jobRequestItemIds": [7, 8],
  "additionalDetails": [ { "field": "Customer", "value": "…" } ] | null
}

The response is 201 { id }: the new order, or the open order the lines were added to.

  • parseCreateOrder (app/lib/orders.ts) requires positive integer jobRequestId and supplierId, and 1–500 unique positive jobRequestItemIds ("Choose between 1 and 500 job lines"). additionalDetails is null or a field list checked by parseOrderDetails (1–50 entries, each a non-blank field of up to 100 characters and value of up to 2,000). It rejects fulfillmentMethod, noteForSupplier and noteForCustomer.
  • createOrder calls create_order(job, supplier, line ids, additional details). When the lines join an open order, the additional details sent are ignored and that order's details stay as they are.
  • Database errors are returned as 400 { error }. Non-admins get 401 or 403.

What each order row copies

Since 2026-10-16 an order belongs to one job, and each row is one job line's selected candidate. There are no allocations: an order never combines several jobs, and one line is never split between orders.

  • One row per candidate. A candidate can be on at most one order row. Once a line's selected candidate is ordered, the line no longer appears in Create order.
  • Several suppliers for one job. Each supplier gets its own order for the job. A line goes to the supplier of its selected candidate.
  • The quantity comes from the line. The row takes the quantity the job line shows available (never more than its Qty), otherwise its Qty. Nothing else limits it.
  • The row is a snapshot. After creation the row belongs to the order. Editing it never changes the candidate or the job line, and editing the candidate never changes the order.

No answer is refused, so an unanswered or unpriced line can be ordered and corrected on the order afterwards.

Changing rows after creation

On the job's order card, Chris can edit a row's name, size, quantity, price, status and both notes. Add row adds a blank row ("New plant", quantity 1, Pending, with no candidate), and the bin deletes a row. Deleting the last row deletes the order too.

How it is stored and enforced
  • orders.job_request_id is NOT NULL with ON DELETE CASCADE. The unique partial index orders_one_open_per_job_supplier on (job_request_id, supplier_id) allows one active New, Processing or Order Ready order per job and supplier.
  • order_items.candidate_id is UNIQUE (order_items_candidate_id_key). orderLineChoices skips any line whose selected candidate already has a row, on any order, open or closed. create_order refuses it too ("… is already on order O-…").
  • Migration 0146 dropped the allocation table order_item_job_request_items (backed up as order_item_job_request_items_pre_0146), along with every remaining-demand, requested-quantity and supplier-capacity check. The customer-confirmed flag and the per-supplier advisory lock went with it. create_order now takes the job row lock, so orders for one job are made one at a time.
  • The job must be active and not closed ("This job request is closed"), and the supplier active ("Active supplier required"). Each line must be an active line of that job, and its selected candidate must be that supplier's ("… is selected from another supplier").

Storage and validation
  • orders.additional_details was renamed from note_for_supplier in 0149 and has been jsonb since 0151: an array of {"field", "value"} objects, kept in display order. The CHECK is_order_details allows NULL or 1–50 objects with exactly those two keys, each a non-blank string (field ≤ 100 characters, value ≤ 2,000). 0151 converted the old markdown rows into fields and kept any other text as a Notes field (old text in orders_pre_0151).
  • orders.change_log (text, 0150) holds markdown bullets. 0150 moved existing logs out of the details. A log written as a pipe table is shown as date: change bullets (changeLogBullets in app/lib/order-share.ts).
  • The admin PATCH /api/orders/:orderId writes additionalDetails and changeLog. The supplier route refuses both with 403 ("Write your note as noteFromSupplier").

Acknowledging and fulfilling an order

After an order is created, the supplier sees it in the Orders section of the supplier portal. A New order has an Acknowledge Received button pinned at the bottom of its page.

The Acknowledge Received dialog

The dialog, titled "Acknowledge Received · <order>", has no fields. It reads: "If anything about this order changes, send Local Plant Supply a message from the Chat sidebar." Its Acknowledge Received button moves the order to Processing and records the confirmation date, shown on the order as "Acknowledged: <date>". The supplier does not enter a date: the target date is the job's EDD, and pickup or delivery is the job's too.

After acknowledgement

  • Mark as Ready moves a Processing order to Order Ready. It asks once more: the first press turns the button into "Confirm: ready for <target date>", and the second press saves.
  • Plant line statuses. On the order page, the supplier can change any row's status. A dialog opens with the status and Your note on this plant, which starts from the saved note and is stored on the row as the supplier's note. Changes are staged first. One Save N status changes button then saves them all and "Sends one update to Rosie."
  • Notes on rows. The supplier can also type straight into a row's Note cell. It saves when they leave the cell, keeps the row's status, and sends Rosie nothing.
  • Prices are LPS's. Qty and Unit price are read-only in the portal. Chris changes them on the job's order card.
Chris acknowledges from the portal view

On the job's Orders tab, a New order shows no Acknowledge button: the code says confirming an order belongs in the supplier portal. When a supplier confirms by phone or email, Chris can acknowledge for them by opening the supplier's portal view of the order (admins can open any supplier's portal). Rosie can also record the confirmation with confirm_order once the details are clear. Once an order is Processing, the job's Orders tab shows Mark as Ready.

Not the same as an AR submission

Submitting availability answers never confirms an order. Order acknowledgement is a separate step on the order itself.

How it is enforced
  • parsePartnerOrderUpdate accepts only status, additionalDetails, changeLog and noteFromSupplier. So both PATCH /api/orders/:orderId and PATCH /api/partner/:id/orders/:orderId answer fulfillmentMethod or fulfillmentDate with 400. parseCreateOrder rejects fulfillmentMethod with "Set pickup/delivery on the job and send additionalDetails".
  • listOrders reads the order's fulfillmentMethod and EDD (jobSchedules) from its job. The tag on the row is the read-only MethodPill (components/workspace/record-row.tsx).
  • GET /api/partner/:id/orders/:orderId returns { order, canEdit: true }.
  • History: migration 0098 allowed confirmation with no method. Migration 0149 dropped orders.fulfillment_method and orders.fulfillment_date (values backed up in orders_pre_0149), and confirming now needs neither.

What suppliers do see

On an order, the supplier sees the job name, its EDD and Pickup/Delivery, the Additional details (which carry the customer, delivery address and contacts), the plants, quantities, prices and statuses, LPS's note on each plant, their own notes and the change log. They do not see LPS's internal job ID or which candidate each row came from. These are left out of the data the server sends, not just hidden on screen. On availability requests, customer details reach the supplier only when "share customer details" is turned on.

Access checks
  • The /api/orders/** routes check role === 'admin' in each handler. Otherwise they return 401 unauthorized or 403 forbidden.
  • The /api/partner/:id/orders/** routes use requirePartnerAccess. Admins can open any supplier. A partner can open only their own supplier or its company's branches (canAccessSupplier). Any other supplier returns 404 rather than 403, so a partner cannot even confirm that another supplier exists. Each order is looked up with that supplier's scope, so another supplier's order is also a 404.
  • Supplier payloads come from listOrders({ forSupplier: true }). They leave out publicToken, jobRequestId, shareJobs and each row's candidateId, and take the job ID and name out of jobSchedules. They include additionalDetails, changeLog and noteFromSupplier, and customerDetails (job name, customer and address). The availability request link never points to a Draft AR.
  • The header and row PATCH routes (admin and portal) and the Add row route refuse a closed order with 409 "This order is closed". Deleting a row from a closed order returns 404. Publishing a closed order returns 400 with the same message.

See Auth, roles and security.

Order cards and screens

Chris's order cards (job Orders tab)

Each order is one row in the same grid as the job cards on /requests. It starts collapsed, and each card expands and collapses on its own.

  • The row: a chevron, the order status pill, the job name (the order reference O-<id> only when the job has no name), the EDD, the job's Pickup/Delivery tag (read-only), the item count, the total and Copy order. On phones the row wraps: status and Copy order, then the name, then EDD · mode · items · total.
  • Under the row: the collapsible Additional details, label over value. Chris edits them there (see Additional details and change log).
  • Expanded: a tinted action bar (Mark as Ready on a Processing order, Save status changes when statuses are staged, the supplier name, "Acknowledged: <date>" and the Availability request link), the supplier's Note from supplier (read-only), then the rows. The rows are drawn like the job page's AR table: Qty, Size, Plant (LPS's note for the supplier beneath it), Status, Note (the supplier's reply), Unit price and Line total. Every value reads as text and becomes a field when clicked (press and hold on touch), saving when you leave it. Each row has a bin (Delete order row); for the last row the confirmation warns "It is the last row, so the order is deleted too." Below the rows are Add row and a collapsible Change log that Chris edits in place.

The Orders tab refreshes whenever the job gets a database change event, without losing an order draft that is still being filled in.

Supplier portal

  • Orders queue (/partner/:id/orders): colour-coded New / Processing / Ready filter buttons with counts. These are multi-select. New and Processing are selected on a fresh visit, and there is no "All" button. A floating selector switches between By order, By status (grouped by plant line status) and By plant.
  • Order cards use the same one-row layout: status, job name, EDD, Pickup/Delivery, item count and total. They show no customer and no note. The whole card is a link to the order page.
  • Order page (/partner/:id/orders/:orderId): it starts with the same row as its heading. A tinted strip under it holds "Your note" (the supplier's note on the order), the Availability request link, a read-only notice when the account cannot edit, and "Acknowledged: <date>". The Additional details follow, open by default. Then come a summary strip of row counts by status with the subtotal, the rows (Qty, Size, Plant with LPS's note beneath, Status, Note, Unit price, Line total) and a collapsed Change log. On phones each row is a card. Acknowledge Received or Mark as Ready is pinned at the bottom.
  • Print sheet: the Print button on the order page prints the job name and one bordered table of Mode, Target date and the Additional details fields, two to a row. Then come Qty, Size, Name (LPS's note under it), Unit price, Total, empty Ready and Need to Order tick boxes for the yard, and an empty Note column to write in, followed by the subtotal. It has no order number, supplier, status, availability request or change log.
  • Order/Request History (/partner/:id/closed-orders): closed orders, shown read-only.
  • Portal order screens refresh on the app's live change signal, when the window gets focus, when the tab becomes visible again, when the browser comes back online, and on a 60-second fallback timer while visible. There is no Refresh button.
No customer-wide order view yet

The Customers pages do not show order totals. To see a customer's orders, open the Orders tab of each of their jobs.

Components and labels
  • OrderCard renders both views. With a supplierId it shows the portal detail layout. Without one it shows Chris's compact card. OrderRow puts an order into RecordRow (components/workspace/record-row.tsx), shared by Chris's card, the portal list and the portal page heading. PartnerOrderCard is the portal list card (RecordLinkCard + RecordRow). OrderStatusFilters and OrderViewControls drive the queue (components/workspace/partner-orders.tsx, where the default is ['new','processing']).
  • Order row status labels (ORDER_ITEM_STATUS_LABELS): Pending, Available, Partial Qty, Unavailable, Diff Sz/Sub (stored as diff_size_sub, renamed from diff_size_qty_sub in 0130), Need to Order and Arriving Soon. The same stored value need_to_order is labelled Can Order on job lines.
  • The EDD on every order row comes from orderEdd: the job's expected_install_date (jobSchedules[0]).
  • orderRecordName returns record_name || 'O-' + id. Per-job names such as JR-42:Order-1 depend on migration 0104. The code reads record_name only if it exists, and the repository does not record 0104 as applied, so expect O-<id>.
  • orderSubtotal adds up the non-null line totals and is empty when no row has a price. Row total is a generated column (quantity × unit_price).
  • Add row calls POST /api/orders/:orderId/items (addOrderItem). The bin calls DELETE /api/orders/:o/items/:i (delete_order_item), which also removes an order left empty.

Copy order and public order links

Copy order

Copy order on Chris's card opens a menu with four choices:

ChoiceWhat the title links toNotes
PublicThe order's public page (/public/orders/<token>)Publishes the order first if it isn't public yet. Not available for closed orders. Copies a formatted table plus a plain-text version.
PrivateThe supplier's portal order page (/partner/<supplier>/orders/<id>)Opening the link requires signing in. Formatted table plus plain text.
TextThe same portal order pagePlain text only.
Print—Prints the supplier's print sheet (see Supplier portal) from the job's card.

Each copy is titled with the job name (O-<id> only when the job has no name). One details table follows: Mode, Target date, then each Additional details field as its own row, which is where the customer and contacts come from. Then come the rows (Qty, Size, Name with LPS's note under it, Unit Price and Total) and the subtotal. The Text copy has one Field: value line per detail, and each row reads qty x size name - note - unit price, with no line total. There is no supplier, order number or change log. A missing mode or target date reads "To be confirmed"; a missing size, price or total reads "—". In a browser without rich clipboard support, the HTML source is copied instead.

Public order links

A public order link lets a supplier without a portal login see one order without signing in. It is read-only.

  • The page shows the order the way a supplier sees it in the portal, labelled "Shared order · Read-only": the plants, quantities, prices, statuses, notes, the EDD and Pickup/Delivery, the Additional details and the change log. It refreshes every 30 seconds and when the window gets focus.
  • It leaves out the availability request link, the job ID and the token itself. The page asks search engines not to index it and sends no referrer.
  • There are no public write actions. Someone with the link cannot acknowledge the order or mark it ready. For a supplier without a login, Chris or Rosie records their confirmation.
  • The link stops working as soon as the order closes, becomes inactive, its supplier is deactivated, or its job closes. Publishing again creates a new link, and old links stay dead.
Anyone with the link can see it

A public order link shows the same Additional details (customer, delivery address and contacts) as the supplier portal. Share it only with the supplier it is meant for.

Public link mechanics
  • PATCH /api/orders/:orderId with exactly { "isPublic": true | false } (admin). Publishing writes a fresh random UUID to orders.public_token. Revoking sets it to null. A closed order cannot be published ("This order is closed"). The database trigger guard_order_public_link (migration 0114) clears the token when an order closes or becomes inactive, and refuses to publish for an inactive supplier.
  • GET /api/public/orders/:token accepts only a UUID-shaped token. It requires an active order that is not Closed Won/Lost, an active supplier, and an order whose job is still open. It checks the token again after reading the order, so a link revoked mid-read stays dead. Responses carry Cache-Control: no-store and Referrer-Policy: no-referrer. An invalid link returns 404 "This link is no longer available".
  • The request gate lets /public/orders/<uuid> and /api/public/orders/<uuid> through without a session.
  • The Copy order menu creates links but cannot revoke them, and no screen offers revocation (order-sharing.tsx and its test were removed). Revoking is done with the API (isPublic: false) or happens automatically when the order closes.

See Supplier portal and public links.

Notifications and live updates

When a supplier changes an order in the portal, the app sends Rosie a short "wake-up" notice. It lands on the order's job thread (jr-<job id>), because an order belongs to one job. Rosie re-reads the order, acknowledges the change in the supplier's conversation and updates the job.

What happenedEventWhat Rosie sees
The supplier acknowledged the order, marked it ready, or changed their own note on the orderorder_updated"<supplier> confirmed | marked ready | updated order O-<id>. Changes: …", with each field's old and new value.
The supplier saved one or more plant row status changesorder_status_saved"<supplier> saved N order status change(s) on order O-<id>.", with each row's status and the supplier's note.
  • A notice is sent only when a value actually changed. The save always happens first. If the notice fails, the supplier's change is still saved, and the failure is only logged.
  • A supplier event never counts as Chris's approval or the customer's acceptance, and it never changes pickup/delivery.
  • LPS → supplier: Rosie has standing permission to tell a supplier about any verified change to a placed order. For a material change (plant, size, quantity, price, pickup ↔ delivery, target date) she adds one dated bullet to the end of the order's Change log (for example - 10/22: Wax myrtle 15 G qty 10 → 12), then messages the supplier: in the portal chat for partners, or by email for other suppliers. A contact or address change updates its Additional details field instead. Events such as acknowledgement are never logged. The Change log is the only order note that keeps a history.
  • New orders do not trigger an app event. The order simply appears in the supplier's portal, and Rosie tells the supplier from the job's thread. Any email or customer message still needs Chris's approval.
  • Live screens: changes to orders and order rows trigger the app's live refresh signal. Chris's screens and the supplier portal both re-read their data when it arrives, with a 60-second fallback timer. Public order pages do not get the live signal; they refresh every 30 seconds and when the window gets focus.
Event delivery details
  • notifySupplierEvent (app/lib/notify-supplier-events.ts) posts to the harness endpoint /webhook/rosie/supplier-events/notify with the shared-secret header x-app-secret (env var SUPPLIER_EVENTS_APP_SECRET) and a 5-second timeout. If the variable is not set, nothing is sent.
  • Every order event carries threadId: "jr-<orders.job_request_id>" (supplierOrderEventContext). The plugin falls back to Rosie's main thread only when an event names no thread.
  • The partner header PATCH compares status and noteFromSupplier with the saved order. It sends order_updated with orderChanges[] (1–2 entries). The harness plugin accepts only those two fields, and status values of processing or order_ready.
  • POST /api/partner/:id/orders/:orderId/status-notification with { changes: [{ itemId, status, note }] } (1–500 changes) sends order_status_saved. The portal calls it after saving each row status one at a time.
  • Only portal routes send events. That includes an admin using the portal view, in which case the notice is still worded as "<supplier> confirmed…". Changes Chris makes from the job Orders tab (/api/orders/**), notes typed into a portal row's Note cell, price edits and public-link views send no event. order_status_updated is defined but nothing sends it.
  • Live refresh: the tables orders and order_items are in JOB_REALTIME_TABLES (app/lib/job-events.ts). The server passes their Supabase Realtime changes on over SSE as data-free {type:"jobs"} signals (/api/chat/events for Chris, /api/partner/:id/events for the portal). The browser waits 200 ms to group a burst into one re-read (useJobUpdates).

See Chat and live updates and Harness plugins.

Closing orders

  • Won / Lost is Chris's call. Rosie closes an order as Closed Won or Closed Lost only on Chris's explicit word. A supplier going quiet does not count as a lost sale, and the app has no button that closes an order.
  • Closing can close the job. When the last open order on a job closes, the job closes too: Won if any of its orders was won, otherwise Lost. A job lost before any order was placed can be closed directly.
  • Archived orders. An order counts as closed, and moves to the portal's Order/Request History, when its own status is Closed Won/Lost or its job is closed, archived or inactive. Closed orders are read-only everywhere.
  • Deleting a job (permanent) also deletes all of its orders.
close_order

close_order(p_order_id, p_status) is a service-only database function, run by Rosie, with no app route. Closing again with the same outcome does nothing. A different outcome raises "Order is unavailable or already closed with a different outcome". It locks the order's job first. When no active order on that job is still New, Processing or Order Ready, it sets the job to closed_won if any of its active orders is Closed Won, otherwise closed_lost, unless the job is already closed or archived. Since 0146 there is no demand-coverage condition. Deleting a job deletes its orders through orders.job_request_id ON DELETE CASCADE and the job-delete trigger, so there are no orders shared with other jobs to keep. The derived "closed" flag is calculated in listOrders each time orders are read. It is not stored.

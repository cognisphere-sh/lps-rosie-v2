LPS Handbook

Local Plant Supply (LPS) helps landscape professionals and homeowners get the plants they need for a job. It sources the plants from supplier nurseries, then prices, quotes and orders them. This handbook explains how that work runs through the LPS app and its four AI agents, from the business view down to the code.

Everyone~3 min read

What this handbook covers

LPS runs on two things working together. One is a custom operations app that the LPS team and supplier partners use in a browser. The other is a team of four AI agents that handle the routine work:

  • Rosie runs jobs, quotes, customer contact, supplier availability requests and orders.
  • Daisy finds and onboards suppliers and keeps their records, price lists and plant aliases up to date.
  • Lily collects feedback and writes requirements.
  • Nova changes and ships the software.

Chris, the owner, makes the business decisions. Rosie needs his yes before she sends an availability request, emails a customer or supplier, picks any candidate other than an exact match, deletes anything or closes a job as Won or Lost. Routine work, such as recording lines and prices or telling a supplier about an order change, needs no approval.

A customer request becomes a Job (JR-<n>), which moves through Quote → Availability → Committed → Won or Lost (stored as quoted, availability, committed, closed_won, closed_lost). Along the way LPS sends each supplier one Availability Request per job (shown as AR-<id>) to confirm stock. Once the customer commits, LPS places a supplier Order (shown as O-<id>), which moves through New → Processing → Order Ready. The handbook explains each of these steps, the rules behind them and the system that carries them out.

The system at a glance

The LPS team uses the app in a browser or as an installed app. Supplier partners sign in to their own company's portal. Other suppliers answer through a shared web link for that availability request or order, with no sign-in. Customers never sign in: LPS contacts them by email. Behind the app sit a Supabase database and the CogniSphere harness, which runs the four agents.

For the detail behind each box, see Architecture.

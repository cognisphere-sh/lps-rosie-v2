Status lifecycles

A job, its supplier questions, each plant line, each supplier order and each supplier login all carry a status, and every status moves only in set ways. This page lists all of them in one place: what each status is called, what moves it, who moves it, and the rules that always hold.

BusinessTechnicalReference~20 min read

In short

LPS tracks six things that change state over time. Each has its own list of statuses, and the database itself enforces most of the rules, so they hold whether a change comes from the app, a supplier or an agent.

  • A job (a customer's request for plants, shown as JR-<n>) moves through sales stages: Quote → Availability → Committed → Won or Lost. Stages can be skipped. Availability and Committed are normally set automatically. Only Chris (the owner) closes a job as Won or Lost, unless closing its last order closes it automatically.
  • An availability request (AR) asks one supplier about one job's plants. It starts as a private Draft, becomes New when published, Processing once the supplier starts answering, and Completed when the supplier presses Submit. Closed is read-only history. An AR is deleted only together with its job. Nothing sends an AR back to New on its own: LPS reopens it explicitly.
  • Each plant line has an availability answer. On the AR it is the supplier's answer (Available, Partial Qty, Unavailable, Diff Sz/Sub, Can Order, Arriving Soon, or Pending). On the job it is the status of the supplier option (candidate) selected for the line, which LPS can overwrite for the customer.
  • A supplier order goes New → Processing (the supplier acknowledges it) → Order Ready, then Closed Won or Closed Lost. It never moves backwards.
  • Candidate selection: each job line has supplier options ("candidates"), starting with a default APS option the database creates with the line. Exactly one is selected at a time, and the line shows that candidate's size, price, quantity and status. Nothing is copied: the line reads them from the candidate.
  • A partner login starts with a generated password that must be changed at first sign-in. Until then, the only page that login can open is "Choose a password".
The rules that matter most

Closing a job (Won, Lost, Archived, or deactivating it) closes its ARs and revokes their public links. Deleting a job deletes its ARs, together with its lines, candidates and orders. Reopening a job never reopens its closed ARs. Someone has to reopen each one by hand. And nothing ever moves a job backwards on its own: automatic events only move it forward.

Job request lifecycle

A job is one customer's request for plants. Its status is the sales stage: where the deal stands with the customer. Supplier-side progress has its own lifecycles (the AR and the order), so the job does not record supplier confirmation. A job can skip stages: a quick quote stops at Quote, and a committed order can go straight from Quote to Committed with no AR at all.

Invariants

  • Events never regress and never reopen. record_job_workflow_event changes a stage only if the job is active and not Won, Lost or Archived, and only forward: availability_sent moves only a Quote job, and customer_confirmed moves only a Quote or Availability job. A job already at Committed stays there when another AR goes out.
  • Committed comes from order creation. Rosie's rules say Committed is "set by order creation, never by hand", and Won/Lost happen "only on Chris's explicit won/lost word". The app's status pill offers all five stages, and neither the app nor the database blocks a manual Committed. These are working rules, not technical locks.
  • Terminal effects. When a job becomes Won, Lost or Archived, or is deactivated:
    • every AR of the job becomes Closed and its public link is revoked;
    • the job's Rosie chat thread is closed (threads.closed_at is set), which hides it from the chat picker and the notification bell. The transcript is kept;
    • its lines can no longer select or unselect candidates ("Open job line not found");
    • no new order can include it ("This job request is closed"), and no candidate can be added to its ARs;
    • an order whose job is closed is treated as closed. It moves to the archive and refuses changes.
  • Reopening a job never reopens its ARs. Each AR must be reopened explicitly, and a revoked public link is never restored. Publishing again creates a fresh link.
  • Deletion is not a status. Deleting a job removes it outright, with its lines, candidates, ARs and orders (see cross-effects). There is no undo.
  • Everything is audited. Every insert and update on a job or line writes a job_request_changelog row, with the actor shown as "This app", "Rosie", "An agent script" or "Claude Code".

Availability request lifecycle

An availability request (AR) asks one supplier about one job's candidates. Price lists don't prove stock, so the AR is how LPS confirms what a supplier really has. Each job has at most one AR per supplier. Asking that supplier again adds lines to the existing AR. ARs are kept: you can close one or return it to Draft, but not delete it. Only deleting its job deletes an AR.

Invariants

  • ARs are not deleted on their own. There is no delete flag and no delete action (DELETE /api/availability-requests/:id answers 405). Database triggers refuse DELETE and TRUNCATE with "Availability requests cannot be deleted. Move the request to Draft to hide it from the supplier." The one exception is deleting the AR's job, which deletes its ARs in the same transaction (0139).
  • One AR per job and supplier. An insert guard (guard_one_ar_per_job_supplier, 0128) refuses a second AR for the same job and supplier, whatever its status. It is a guard rather than a unique index because a few older pairs predate the rule and are kept. Asking again adds to the existing AR: a Draft is published and a New AR takes the lines. A Processing or Completed AR takes them only after LPS confirms Reopen and add, which reopens it as New first. A Closed AR is refused until someone reopens it ("AR-<id> with this supplier is closed — reopen it to ask for more").
  • Each AR belongs to one job (availability_requests.job_request_id, NOT NULL since 0139; deleting the job deletes the AR). Its candidates must all be lines of that job and must come from the AR's supplier.
  • Drafts never reach the supplier. Every supplier-facing read and every answer function refuses a draft, and the guard revokes the link whenever an AR is in Draft or Closed.
  • Closed is read-only. Candidate writes on a closed AR are refused ("This request is closed or unavailable"), except LPS's own line edits through update_job_line. Adding candidates and setting the note are refused too.
  • Reopen is explicit. No event reopens an AR or returns it to New automatically: not reopening the job, not an LPS edit, not a new line. Adding to a Processing or Completed AR needs a confirmed reopen first.
  • Public link rules. A link is minted on publish for non-Partners. Making an AR public always issues a fresh token; making it private clears the token. A draft cannot be made public ("Publish this draft before sharing it"), and a closed AR is refused too. A link requires an active supplier.
  • Only a submitted answer counts. Rosie is woken by availability_submitted, not by individual saves. A supplier's saved but unsubmitted answer is not acted on.

Side effects of submission

  1. Selected lines switch to an offered substitute. On an open Quote or Availability job, a line whose selected candidate was answered Unavailable with a substitute since the previous submission now selects that substitute, so it reads Diff Sz/Sub (see line statuses).
  2. Other alternatives are hidden. The supplier's other alternatives on this AR's lines, asked or not, are hidden from LPS (hide_from_lps). The line's default and selection, the supplier's originals and the substitutes they offer stay visible. A candidate LPS unhid can be hidden again by a later submission. Committed and closed jobs are not touched; Rosie reports the answer instead.
  3. Rosie is notified. The app posts availability_submitted to Rosie's supplier-events webhook, on a best-effort basis.

Submission no longer copies supplier replies into customer notes. A candidate's customer note follows the supplier's reply unless LPS has written its own, and a new reply clears LPS's note (0132).

How the admin status pill writes, and why nothing resets an AR to New
  • setAvailabilityRequestStatus(arId, status) accepts only the five stored values. For new on a draft it calls send_availability_request (publish); on a Processing, Completed or Closed AR it calls reopen_availability_request; on a New AR it does nothing. For any other target except draft it adds .neq('status','draft'), so a draft can leave only through publish.
  • Stamping: submitted sets submitted_at = now(); draft clears it; a reopen, processing and closed leave it alone. The next submission compares answers against it. The guard also stamps submitted_at whenever the status enters submitted.
  • Migration 0130 dropped the automatic resets: the candidates_processing and candidates_reopen triggers (functions process_availability_answer and availability_request_reopen_on_new_line) are gone, and guard_ar_public_link no longer touches the status when the request-wide note or the share-customer-details flag changes. It only clears submitted_at in Draft, revokes the link in Draft and Closed, requires an active supplier for a link, and refuses Draft → Processing/Completed and any status other than Draft or Closed while the job is closed, inactive or missing.
  • answer_availability_request moves a published AR to processing for a supplier answer. edit_availability_request is the admin's answer path: it runs the same validation with lps.ar_admin_edit on, may answer a draft, and keeps the AR's status.
  • add_candidates_to_availability_request refuses a submitted, processing or closed AR ("AR-<id> is <status>: reopen it before adding candidates"), so the app reopens first when LPS confirms. It clears any earlier supplier answer on the candidate it attaches.

AR item and job line availability

Every plant line has an availability status in two places, and the two vocabularies are kept separate on purpose. On the AR, the status is the supplier's answer about their stock. On the job, it is LPS's decision about the customer's line. A job line has no status of its own: it shows the stored status of its selected supplier option (candidate), and when LPS sets the line's status by hand, that is written to the selected candidate.

Rules for AR item answers

  • Who answers: the supplier in the portal or through the public link, or the admin on the supplier's behalf (for phone and email replies). An admin answer keeps the AR's status; only a supplier's own answer moves it to Processing.
  • When answers are allowed: the AR must not be Closed, and suppliers cannot answer a Draft. The admin may prepare answers on a Draft.
  • A status never changes the price. Only an explicit price edit does. Unavailable forces the quantity to 0.
  • A substitute becomes its own row. When a substitute is named, the asked row is stored Unavailable (quantity 0, price kept) and reads Diff Sz/Sub. The substitute joins the same AR as diff_size_sub at the line's demand and becomes visible to LPS. A price, quantity or reply the supplier then enters on that line goes to the substitute, not the asked row. A typed substitute creates a new, unpriced candidate.
  • Replacing a substitute: an earlier typed substitute is retired. An earlier picked one is reset to unanswered and removed from the AR.
  • Diff Sz/Sub must name something. Saving diff_size_sub on an asked row without a substitute fails with "Pick or describe the substitute".
  • Answers are dated (answered_at). Submission uses that date to decide which answers are new since the last submission.

Display helpers, the job card candidate pill, and order line statuses
  • coverOf(demand, avail) reads the quantity pair on its own: unanswered when Avail is NULL, none when Avail ≤ 0, partial when Avail < Demand, else full. itemPill keeps the stored label but tints a short Available line yellow. coverColor dims an unanswered Avail figure, and shows none in red and partial in amber.

  • The portal's "By status" board matches the raw stored status, so a current substitute (stored unavailable with an offer) lands on the Unavailable card.
  • Order lines are snapshots. create_order copies the selected candidate's status in the line vocabulary (NULL becomes pending and can_order becomes need_to_order). order_items_status_check uses diff_size_sub, so a Diff Sz/Sub line can be ordered. After creation, the supplier or admin can set any of the seven order line values (update_partner_order_item); there is no transition rule between them. The partner portal stages several line changes and sends Rosie one order_status_saved notification.

Supplier order lifecycle

A supplier order is created only after the customer has committed. It belongs to one job and one supplier: Chris ticks the job's lines whose selected candidate is that supplier's, and each becomes one order row copied from its candidate. A job has at most one open order per supplier, so more lines go onto the open one. Pickup/delivery and the target date are the job's. Once created, an order only moves forward: the supplier acknowledges it, then marks it ready, and Rosie closes it as won or lost.

Invariants

  • Forward only. The only status changes the update function accepts are New → Processing and Processing → Order Ready. The app accepts only processing or order_ready in an update ("Choose Processing or Order Ready").
  • Pickup/delivery and the date are the job's. Since 0149 an order has no fulfillment date or method of its own: it shows the job's fulfillment_method and expected install date (EDD).
  • Suppliers write only the status and their own reply. A partner request that includes additionalDetails or changeLog gets 403. One with any other unknown field, such as fulfillmentMethod or fulfillmentDate, gets 400.
  • Closed is final. A closed order, or one whose job is closed, refuses all changes ("Closed orders cannot be changed"; routes answer 409 "This order is closed"). It cannot be made public while closed. Closing it Won or Lost also clears its public link in the database (guard_order_public_link, 0114); an order closed only because its job closed keeps its token, but the public page stops serving it.
  • The job stays Committed. Jobs have no "confirmed" stage. Supplier acknowledgment is recorded only on the order.
  • One row per candidate. order_items.candidate_id is unique, so a candidate is on at most one order row, and a Closed Lost order's candidates cannot be ordered again. Rows added by hand have no candidate. The allocation table and its remaining-demand checks were dropped in 0146.
Admin cards show no Acknowledge button

On the job's Orders tab a New order shows no confirm action, because acknowledging belongs to the supplier. Chris can still acknowledge on the supplier's behalf through the partner-portal preview, which uses the supplier route.

Candidate selection lifecycle

A candidate is one specific supplier option for one job line: a particular plant, at a particular size, from a particular supplier, at a particular price. One candidate per supplier is normally the customer's original request; the others are alternatives. Every line also has a default candidate: the APS original the database creates, unpriced, when the line is inserted. A candidate is asked once it joins an AR, answered once the supplier replies, and selected when Chris drags it onto the line's Selected cell, at which point the line shows its values. A line always has exactly one selection; with nothing else chosen, it is the default.

Invariants

  • Select and unselect work only on an active line of an active job that is not Won, Lost or Archived ("Open job line not found"), and the candidate must belong to that line.
  • A line has exactly one selected candidate. Selecting a different one replaces it, and unselecting, binning or deactivating it returns the line to its default.
  • The default candidate stays an active, visible APS original while its line exists ("The line's default candidate stays its active, visible APS original").
  • A candidate is one fixed plant and size. Suppliers cannot rewrite the size; a different size is a substitute.
  • Every candidate belongs to a job line (job_request_item_id NOT NULL since 0139). A candidate on an AR must have the AR's supplier and belong to the AR's job.
  • Rosie selects only an exact match, or what Chris's direction or a standing rule picks. Any other selection waits for Chris. She never asks which alternative to use; she records alternatives as candidates and Chris drags.
How update_job_line writes the selected candidate

There is no sync trigger any more: 0130 dropped job_request_items_candidate_sync, sync_job_item_to_candidate (and with it 0129's promotion), apply_candidate_to_job_item and link_candidate_offer. An LPS line edit calls update_job_line(line, changes), which takes only status, unit_price, available_quantity and note_for_customer and writes them to the selected candidate (latest body 0152):

Line set toCandidate stores
pendingNULL; Avail cleared
need_to_ordercan_order; Avail = demand unless a quantity is sent
available / arriving_soonthe same value; Avail = demand unless a quantity is sent
partial_qtypartial_qty, keeping its Avail
unavailableunavailable; Avail 0
diff_size_subdiff_size_sub, Avail = demand unless a quantity is sent, even on an original (0152). Exception: an original answered Unavailable with a live substitute stays unavailable, so the supplier's offer is kept.

A quantity sent with the edit wins, and flips Available below demand to Partial Qty (and Partial Qty at full demand back to Available). Leaving Unavailable releases the substitute the candidate offered: a picked one goes back to unanswered and leaves the AR, and a typed one is retired. A price of '' stores NULL and 0 stores $0.00. The function sets lps.lps_override so the closed-AR guard lets the write through; it never touches the AR's status. size or name keys are refused (0145).

Partner account lifecycle (must change password)

Suppliers with a portal login are LPS Partners. Logins are created by the admin, belong to the supplier company (never a single farm), and start with a generated password that is shown once. The partner has to choose their own password the first time they sign in. Until they do, every page sends them to "Choose a password" and every API call is refused.

Invariants

  • Partner status is derived. suppliers.is_lps_partner is true exactly while the company has at least one portal login. A database trigger maintains it, and nothing in the app writes it. Creating the first login makes the supplier a Partner. Deleting the last one turns that off, and from then on its ARs get public links when they are published.
  • Accounts belong to the company (the head branch). Creating a login from a branch's page attaches it to the head branch (partnerScopeId), and a database trigger rejects a login linked to a branch.
  • Passwords are never stored by the app and a generated one can be read only once. The admin cannot see an existing password, only reset it.
  • The gate reads the cookie. After a reset, a session already open keeps its old cookie until it next signs in. The old password no longer works, so the next sign-in carries the flag again.
  • Admin accounts are created out of band by an operator script and do not get the flag.

Full detail on sessions, cookies and the request gate is on Auth, roles and security.

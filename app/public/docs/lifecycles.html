Status lifecycles

A job, its supplier questions, each plant line, each supplier order and each supplier login all carry a status, and every status moves only in set ways. This page lists all of them in one place: what each status is called, what moves it, who moves it, and the rules that always hold.

BusinessTechnicalReference~20 min read

In short

LPS tracks six things that change state over time. Each has its own list of statuses, and the database itself enforces most of the rules, so they hold whether a change comes from the app, a supplier or an agent.

  • A job (a customer's request for plants, shown as JR-<n>) moves through sales stages: Quote → Availability → Committed → Won or Lost. Stages can be skipped. Availability and Committed are normally set automatically. Only Chris (the owner) closes a job as Won or Lost, unless closing its last order closes it automatically.
  • An availability request (AR) asks one supplier about one job's plants. It starts as a private Draft, becomes New when published, Processing once the supplier starts answering, and Completed when the supplier presses Submit. Closed is read-only history. ARs are never deleted, and any change LPS makes to a published AR sends it back to New.
  • Each plant line has an availability answer. On the AR it is the supplier's answer (Available, Partial Qty, Unavailable, Diff Sz/Sub, Can Order, Arriving Soon, or Pending). On the job it is LPS's decision for the customer, which usually comes from the supplier option Chris picked.
  • A supplier order goes New → Processing (the supplier acknowledges it and sets a fulfillment date) → Order Ready, then Closed Won or Closed Lost. It never moves backwards.
  • Candidate selection: each job line has supplier options ("candidates"). One of them can be selected, and the line then copies its size, price, quantity and status.
  • A partner login starts with a generated password that must be changed at first sign-in. Until then, the only page that login can open is "Choose a password".
The three rules that matter most

Closing a job (Won, Lost, or deleting it) closes its ARs and revokes their public links. Reopening a job never reopens those ARs. Someone has to reopen each one by hand. And nothing ever moves a job backwards on its own: automatic events only move it forward.

Job request lifecycle

A job is one customer's request for plants. Its status is the sales stage: where the deal stands with the customer. Supplier-side progress has its own lifecycles (the AR and the order), so the job does not record supplier confirmation. A job can skip stages: a quick quote stops at Quote, and a committed order can go straight from Quote to Committed with no AR at all.

Invariants

  • Events never regress and never reopen. record_job_workflow_event changes a stage only if the job is active and not Won, Lost or Archived, and only forward: availability_sent moves only a Quote job, and customer_confirmed moves only a Quote or Availability job. A job already at Committed stays there when another AR goes out.
  • Committed comes from order creation. Rosie's rules say Committed is "set by order creation, never by hand", and Won/Lost happen "only on Chris's explicit won/lost word". The app's status pill offers all five stages, and neither the app nor the database blocks a manual Committed. These are working rules, not technical locks.
  • Terminal effects. When a job becomes Won, Lost or Archived, or is deactivated:
    • every AR of the job becomes Closed and its public link is revoked;
    • the job's Rosie chat thread is closed (threads.closed_at is set), which hides it from the chat picker and the notification bell. The transcript is kept;
    • its lines can no longer select or unselect candidates ("Open job line not found");
    • no new order can include it ("This job request is closed"), and no candidate can be added to its ARs;
    • an order whose every linked job is closed is treated as closed. It moves to the archive and refuses changes.
  • Reopening a job never reopens its ARs. Each AR must be reopened explicitly, and a revoked public link is never restored. Publishing again creates a fresh link.
  • Deletion is not a status. Deleting a job removes it outright (see cross-effects). There is no undo.
  • Everything is audited. Every insert and update on a job or line writes a job_request_changelog row, with the actor shown as "This app", "Rosie", "An agent script" or "Claude Code".

An availability request (AR) asks one supplier about one job's candidates. Price lists don't prove stock, so the AR is how LPS confirms what a supplier really has. Each job has at most one AR per supplier. Asking that supplier again adds lines to the existing AR. ARs are kept permanently: you can close one or return it to Draft, but never delete it.

Invariants

  • ARs are never deleted. There is no delete flag and no delete action (DELETE /api/availability-requests/:id answers 405). Database triggers refuse DELETE and TRUNCATE with "Availability requests cannot be deleted. Move the request to Draft to hide it from the supplier."
  • One AR per job and supplier. An insert guard (guard_one_ar_per_job_supplier, 0128) refuses a second AR for the same job and supplier, whatever its status. It is a guard rather than a unique index because a few older pairs predate the rule and are kept. Asking again adds to the existing AR: a Draft is published, a Processing or Completed AR goes back to New, and a Closed AR is refused until someone reopens it ("AR-<id> with this supplier is closed — reopen it to ask for more").
  • Each AR belongs to one job (availability_requests.job_request_id, set to NULL if the job is deleted). Its candidates must all be lines of that job and must come from the AR's supplier.
  • Drafts never reach the supplier. Every supplier-facing read and every answer function refuses a draft, and the guard revokes the link whenever an AR is in Draft or Closed.
  • Closed is read-only. Candidate writes on a closed AR are refused ("This request is closed or unavailable"), except LPS's own line-sync edits. Adding candidates and setting the note are refused too.
  • Reopen is explicit. Neither reopening a job nor any other event reopens an AR automatically.
  • Public link rules. A link is minted on publish for non-Partners. Making an AR public always issues a fresh token; making it private clears the token. A draft cannot be made public ("Publish this draft before sharing it"), and a closed AR is refused too. A link requires an active supplier.
  • Only a submitted answer counts. Rosie is woken by availability_submitted, not by individual saves. A supplier's saved but unsubmitted answer is not acted on.

Side effects of submission

  1. Supplier replies become customer notes. For every candidate answered since the previous submission, a nonblank note_from_supplier is copied into note_for_customer.
  2. Selected lines are re-applied. Every active line on an open Quote or Availability job that selects one of those answered candidates is re-derived from it (see line statuses). Lines that select nothing are untouched. Committed and closed jobs are not updated; Rosie reports the answer instead.
  3. Rosie is notified. The app posts availability_submitted to Rosie's supplier-events webhook, on a best-effort basis.
How the admin status pill writes, and which session flags suppress the reset to New
  • setAvailabilityRequestStatus(arId, status) accepts only the five stored values. For new on a draft it calls send_availability_request (publish) instead of updating directly. For any other target except draft it adds .neq('status','draft'), so a draft can leave only through publish.
  • Stamping: submitted sets submitted_at = now(); new and draft clear it; processing and closed leave it alone. The guard also stamps submitted_at whenever the status enters submitted.
  • process_availability_answer (AFTER UPDATE on candidates) compares the row without updated_at and answered_at. Any difference resets a non-draft, non-closed AR to new and clears submitted_at. It does nothing while lps.ar_submission or lps.ar_supplier_answer is on, or while lps.applying_offer is on without lps.line_sync. A line-sync edit therefore does reset a published AR to New.
  • guard_ar_public_link resets to new on a change to note_for_supplier or share_customer_details, except while lps.ar_supplier_note is on (the supplier's own request-wide note on Submit).
  • availability_request_reopen_on_new_line moves processing/submitted to new when a candidate joins, except while applying an offer or a supplier answer (a substitute row joining the AR does not count as an LPS change).
  • edit_availability_request is the admin's answer path. It runs the same validation as the supplier path with lps.ar_admin_edit on: a draft stays draft, and a published AR goes to new, not processing.

AR item and job line availability

Every plant line has an availability status in two places, and the two vocabularies are kept separate on purpose. On the AR, the status is the supplier's answer about their stock. On the job, it is LPS's decision about the customer's line. The job line normally takes its status from the supplier option (candidate) Chris selected, but LPS can also set it by hand.

Rules for AR item answers

  • Who answers: the supplier in the portal or through the public link, or the admin on the supplier's behalf (for phone and email replies). An admin answer resets a published AR to New rather than Processing.
  • When answers are allowed: the AR must not be Closed, and suppliers cannot answer a Draft. The admin may prepare answers on a Draft.
  • A status never changes the price. Only an explicit price edit does. Unavailable forces the quantity to 0.
  • A substitute becomes its own row. When a substitute is named, the asked row is stored Unavailable (quantity 0, price kept) and reads Diff Sz/Sub. The substitute row on the same AR becomes Available, or Partial Qty if an offered quantity is below demand. It takes the offered price if one was given, else its own price (the app's dialog sends neither a price nor a quantity, so it gets its own price and the demand). It is hidden from AR line counts but stays linked so it can be ordered. A typed substitute creates a new candidate.
  • Replacing a substitute: an earlier typed substitute is retired. An earlier picked one is reset to unanswered and removed from the AR.
  • Diff Sz/Sub must name something. Saving diff_size_qty_sub without a substitute fails with "Pick or describe the substitute".
  • Answers are dated (answered_at). Submission uses that date to decide which answers are new since the last submission.

Supplier order lifecycle

A supplier order is created only after the customer has committed. It can take lines the supplier answered on an AR, or the quoted job lines directly (a "direct order"). One order can combine several jobs, and one job line can be split across several orders. Once created, an order only moves forward: the supplier acknowledges it with a fulfillment date, then marks it ready, and Rosie closes it as won or lost.

Invariants

  • Forward only. The only status changes the update function accepts are New → Processing and Processing → Order Ready. The app accepts only processing or order_ready in an update ("Choose Processing or Order Ready").
  • The fulfillment date belongs to the supplier. It cannot be set at creation, it is required to enter Processing or Order Ready, and once the order is in either state it cannot be cleared. It is never copied from the job's EDD.
  • Pickup/delivery is admin-only and optional. A supplier request that includes it gets 403. Suppliers can confirm and mark ready while it is unset.
  • Closed is final. A closed order, or one whose every linked job is closed, refuses all changes ("Closed orders cannot be changed"; routes answer 409 "This order is closed"). It cannot be made public while closed. Closing it Won or Lost also clears its public link in the database (guard_order_public_link, 0114); an order closed only because its jobs closed keeps its token, but the public page stops serving it.
  • The job stays Committed. Jobs have no "confirmed" stage. Supplier acknowledgment is recorded only on the order.
  • Quantity caps. No job line is ordered beyond the customer's demand across all suppliers, nor beyond the supplier's offered quantity (except Can Order). Lost and inactive orders release their quantity. Pending and Unavailable AR answers cannot be ordered, and neither can an answer that offered a substitute (order the substitute row instead).

Candidate selection lifecycle

A candidate is one specific supplier option for one job line: a particular plant, at a particular size, from a particular supplier, at a particular price. One candidate per supplier is normally the customer's original request; the others are alternatives. A candidate is asked once it joins an AR, answered once the supplier replies, and selected when Chris drags it onto the line's Selected cell, at which point the line takes its values.

Invariants

  • Select and unselect work only on an active line of an active job that is not Won, Lost or Archived ("Open job line not found"), and the candidate must belong to that line.
  • A line has at most one selected candidate. Selecting a different one replaces it.
  • A candidate is one fixed plant and size. Suppliers cannot rewrite the size; a different size is a substitute.
  • A candidate on an AR must have the AR's supplier and belong to the AR's job.
  • Rosie selects only an exact match, or what Chris's direction or a standing rule picks. Any other selection waits for Chris. She never asks which alternative to use; she records alternatives as candidates and Chris drags.
Line → candidate status mapping used by the sync trigger

When the line's status changes while a candidate is selected, the candidate's status is set as follows (0129):

Partner account lifecycle (must change password)

Suppliers with a portal login are LPS Partners. Logins are created by the admin, belong to the supplier company (never a single farm), and start with a generated password that is shown once. The partner has to choose their own password the first time they sign in. Until they do, every page sends them to "Choose a password" and every API call is refused.

States

StateStored asWhat the user can do
No loginNo app_users row for the company; suppliers.is_lps_partner = falseThe supplier gets ARs only through public links by email.
Must change passwordmust_change_password = true; the session cookie carries p: trueOnly /change-password and the auth API (/api/auth/*), plus the pages that are public to everyone (sign-in, public links). Other pages redirect to /change-password; other API calls get 403 password change required.
Activemust_change_password = falseThe company's own portal (/partner/<id>) only. Other companies' ids answer 404.
DeletedAuth user removed; the app_users row cascadesNothing. The username is free again.

From → ToTriggerActorImplemented in

Must change password → Active"Choose a password": current password, new password (at least 10 characters, different from the current one), confirmation.The partnerPOST /api/auth/password → changeOwnPassword; clears the flag and re-issues the session cookie without it

Any → Deleted"Delete this login" (asks first; cannot be undone).AdminDELETE /api/suppliers/:id/partner-account → deletePartnerAccount

Invariants

  • Partner status is derived. suppliers.is_lps_partner is true exactly while the company has at least one portal login. A database trigger maintains it, and nothing in the app writes it. Creating the first login makes the supplier a Partner. Deleting the last one turns that off, and from then on its ARs get public links when they are published.
  • Accounts belong to the company (the head branch). Creating a login from a branch's page attaches it to the head branch (partnerScopeId), and a database trigger rejects a login linked to a branch.
  • Passwords are never stored by the app and a generated one can be read only once. The admin cannot see an existing password, only reset it.
  • The gate reads the cookie. After a reset, a session already open keeps its old cookie until it next signs in. The old password no longer works, so the next sign-in carries the flag again.
  • Admin accounts are created out of band by an operator script and do not get the flag.

Full detail on sessions, cookies and the request gate is on Auth, roles and security.

Chat and live updates

The LPS team talks to the AI agents in a chat panel inside the app, with a separate conversation for each job, supplier and customer. Suppliers chat with Local Plant Supply from their portal, and Rosie passes each message to the job it is about. When anything changes in the database, every open screen gets a short "something changed" signal and reloads itself, so nobody has to press refresh.

BusinessEngineersTechnical~25 min read

In short

If you read only this section, these are the main points:

  • Who you chat with. The chat panel lets the team talk to three agents: Daisy (suppliers and catalogue), Rosie (customers, jobs and orders) and Nova (app developer). Lily (product) is listed too, but she is reached on Telegram, not in the panel. See Meet the agents.
  • Each conversation has a subject. Every agent has a general conversation called Main. Rosie also keeps one conversation per job request, one per supplier company and one per customer. Daisy keeps one per supplier company. The Conversations list in the panel shows all of them, grouped and searchable.
  • How a message gets there. You type in the app. The app checks that you are a signed-in team member, saves the message in the database and wakes the agent on that conversation. The agent's reply is saved to the same conversation and appears in your panel.
  • Screens keep themselves up to date. The database tells the app server the moment something changes. The app server sends a tiny "something changed" signal to every open browser, and each browser reloads what it shows. The signal never carries the data itself. If the live connection drops, screens fall back to slow automatic refreshes (every 10 to 60 seconds), so a missed signal costs at most a minute.
  • Unread dots are shared across devices. "Read" is saved on the server, not in the browser. Opening a conversation on your phone clears its dot on your laptop too. The bell in the header lists every unread conversation and can mark them all read.
  • Files, voice notes and quick questions. You can attach files, paste images or record a voice note. Agents often ask multiple-choice questions as interactive cards; you answer them one at a time and press Submit once.
  • A job's chat has tabs. On a job, the panel shows a tab for Rosie (the team's own conversation about the job), one for the customer and one for each supplier on the job. A supplier or customer tab shows that company's conversation exactly as they see it, across all their jobs, with a switch to show only this job.
  • Suppliers chat with Local Plant Supply from the portal. A supplier sees only their own messages and what LPS sent them, all signed "Local Plant Supply", each tagged with the job it is about. Whatever a team member types in a supplier's or customer's conversation goes to that party as LPS (to a partner's portal; a customer or non-partner supplier has no portal, so it is only recorded). The team's discussion with Rosie stays on the job's Rosie tab and is never shown to the supplier.
  • Supplier actions wake Rosie. When a supplier submits availability answers or confirms or updates an order, the app rings a "doorbell" that wakes Rosie on the conversation of the job that availability request or order belongs to. Saving answers without pressing Submit does not wake her. If the agent server is down, nothing is lost: the database already holds everything, only the wake-up is missed.
Good to know

A closed job's conversation disappears from the Conversations list and the bell, but its history is kept and comes back if the job is reopened. A permanently deleted job's conversation is deleted with it and cannot be recovered.

Conversation id validation

Before a message is sent or a conversation is cleared, the app checks the id with validCanonicalThreadId() in app/lib/chat-request.ts. It never repairs input; it only accepts or rejects:

/^(?:main|jr-[1-9]\d*|(?:supplier|customer)-[\p{L}\p{N}]+(?:[._-][\p{L}\p{N}]+)*)$/u
  • a non-empty string, already trimmed, already NFC-normalised and already lowercase
  • at most 160 characters (THREAD_ID_MAX_CHARS) and 256 UTF-8 bytes (THREAD_ID_MAX_UTF8_BYTES); anything over 320 code units is rejected before any normalisation work
  • no control characters
  • matches the pattern above
Watch out: other thread ids are read-only from the panel

The harness also has conversations the pattern does not cover, such as router, email-derived threads and agent task threads (<parent>-§-[TASK]-§-<slug>). The validator runs only on send and reset. A panel opened on such a thread through a deep link can read its transcript, but sending or clearing is rejected with 400 invalid conversation.

How a message travels

Here is what happens between pressing Send and seeing the agent's reply, in plain terms:

  1. You send. Your message appears straight away (greyed as pending) and goes to the app server.
  2. The app checks you. Only signed-in team members (admins) may chat. The app forwards the message to the agent's chat inbox on the harness, identifying itself with a server-side secret and your username.
  3. The harness saves and wakes. The webchat plugin saves the message to the database, then wakes the agent on that conversation with your text, any attachments and the page you were on. In a supplier's or customer's conversation the message is for the party instead: Rosie is only told about it silently and owes no reply.
  4. The agent works. The panel shows "Rosie is working…" while the agent is actually running on the conversation you are looking at.
  5. The agent replies. The agent sends its reply with a small command-line tool. The plugin saves it to the same conversation.
  6. Your screen updates. The database change triggers a live signal; your panel fetches the new message and shows it.

1. Browser → app

useChat().send in chat.tsx:

  1. Drops the send unless the panel's bound destination (agentId, threadId, revision) is still the committed one, and refuses while a clear (reset) lock is held for that conversation.
  2. Appends an optimistic message with a negative id (senderKind: 'lps'). It does not set pending: "working…" comes only from the server (see below).
  3. POST /api/chat/<agent>/send with { text, attachments, threadId, page }, plus jobRequestId when the conversation was opened as a job's supplier or customer tab, bounded at 20 s (SEND_TIMEOUT_MS). page comes from currentPage() in app/lib/chat-page.ts: { path: pathname + search, title }.
  4. On success, the returned { id } replaces the optimistic row (if a poll already brought that id in, the polled row is kept) and the cursor advances.
  5. On failure, the optimistic row is removed and the panel shows "Message failed to send." If the request was aborted instead (the 20 s bound fired, or you switched conversation), the row is removed with no error text.

2. App proxy → harness

app/app/api/chat/[...path]/route.ts does, in order:

  1. Auth first. resolveUser(cookie) must return role === 'admin', otherwise 403 {"error":"forbidden"}, before any body parsing, queueing or upstream call. (app/proxy.ts has already turned away signed-out callers with 401 and partners with 403.)
  2. Path shape. Exactly [agentId, action], with agentId in CHAT_AGENTS and action in messages, send, upload, file, reset, threads, read. Anything else is 404.
  3. Send/reset only: the body must be JSON (400 invalid json), an object, and carry a threadId that passes validCanonicalThreadId (400 invalid conversation). The call is then queued per conversation (see Ordering).
  4. Forward to ${HARNESS_URL}/webhook/<agent>/webchat/api/<action><query> (default HARNESS_URL is http://127.0.0.1:3142) with authorization: Bearer <HARNESS_APP_SECRET> and x-app-user: <username>, plus the browser's content-type and x-file-name. Bodies are streamed (duplex: 'half', cache: 'no-store'). The upstream status and body come back unchanged, with content-type and content-disposition.

3. Webchat plugin receives the message

Browser-side plugin routes first check identity: the plugin replays the incoming cookie, authorization and x-app-user headers to the harness's GET /api/auth/me and accepts the call only if a non-null user comes back. If the harness cannot be reached it fails closed (401 unauthorized).

POST api/send then:

  • defaults threadId to the config value (main), trims text, and rejects empty text with no attachments (400);
  • checks each attachment's stored name and sanitises its label and MIME type;
  • calls deliver() (below) with senderKind: "lps" and your username as senderName, then marks the message read for the team (your own message never counts as unread);
  • answers { ok: true, id }.

  1. If the job's row doesn't exist yet (a reserved job), the insert is retried without job_request_id. Any other failed insert fails the send, and no agent wake-up happens.
  2. This block goes only to the agent; the saved transcript keeps only what was typed.
  3. Wake the agent: ctx.notify("web_message" | "web_message_silent", { text, channelId, threadIdOverride: eventThread, isSilent?, metadata }). Metadata: senderName, senderKind, messageId, webchatThreadId; jobRequestId when the message is tagged; for web messages originThreadId and originChannel: "web"; for portal messages supplierId and supplierName; and attachments and page when present. The harness renders these PascalCased in the agent's <harness-metadata> block (SenderKind, JobRequestId, …).
  4. Mark pending: threads.pending_since = now, except for a silent delivery.

pageContext() accepts only a path starting with / (not //), collapses whitespace and caps the path at 500 and the title at 200 characters. The agent is told that Page is context, not a verified identity.

4. The agent replies

The agent never writes to the database directly. It runs the seeded CLI scripts/webchat/webchat-cli:

webchat-cli send --text "…" [--file <path>]… [--thread-id <id>] [--portal] [--job <id>]
webchat-cli send --log-as supplier|customer --sender "<name>" --thread-id <party thread> --text "…"
webchat-cli ask  --questions-file <path.json> [--thread-id <id>] [--portal] [--job <id>]
webchat-cli route --thread-id <party thread> --message <id> --to jr-<id>|main [--to …] [--note "…"]
webchat-cli --self-check
  • --thread-id defaults to $PI_THREAD_ID, the conversation the agent's turn is running on.
  • The CLI always sends originThreadId ($PI_THREAD_ID), so a message a job thread writes into a supplier's conversation records where it came from. --job names the job explicitly; otherwise the plugin derives jobRequestId from the origin (or target) thread when it is jr-<id>.
  • --portal marks a message party-visible: a partner's portal and the panel's supplier/customer tabs show only these and the party's own messages.
  • --log-as supplier|customer --sender "<name>" records a message the party sent elsewhere (an email) in their conversation, as role: "user" with that senderKind. It wakes nobody, never reaches a portal and leaves pending_since alone.
  • The inbox URL is $HARNESS_BASE_URL/webhook/$PI_AGENT_ID/webchat when that is set, else $PI_WEBHOOK_BASE/webchat. There is no hard-coded port.
  • send and ask POST { text, attachments, questionnaire?, threadId?, portal?, originThreadId?, jobRequestId?, logAs?, senderName? } to api/reply; route POSTs { threadId, messageId, fromThreadId, to: [...], note? } to api/route. Both carry x-webhook-secret: $COGNISPHERE_WEBHOOK_SECRET. A non-200 exits non-zero.

POST api/reply checks the webhook secret (when the env var is set; 401 otherwise), validates and encodes any questionnaire, requires attachment paths to resolve inside the agent's directory (400 attachment outside agent dir) and copies them to state/outbox/. It records the message as role: "agent", senderKind: "agent" (or, for --log-as, role: "user" with the party's kind and name), plus portal: true with --portal, its originThreadId and its job, and clears pending_since (not for a log). It answers { ok, id, threadId, jobRequestId? }. It moves neither read cursor: a reply is exactly what the unread dot is for.

POST api/route (same webhook secret; 404 unless partyRouting is on) is how a supplier's or customer's own thread hands their message on. It takes a supplier-/customer- threadId, a messageId that must be the party's own message, and up to 5 targets, each main or jr-<id>. It tags the message with origin_thread_id = the first target (and that target's job), then delivers it to every target with context "Routed here by <thread>", any other targets and the optional note.

5. Back to the browser

  1. The reply's INSERT fires the preview trigger, which UPDATEs the threads row.
  2. Supabase Realtime delivers the change to the app server's channel; the relay turns it into a thread event (see SSE event types).
  3. GET /api/chat/events pushes it to every team browser.
  4. An open panel whose agent and conversation match exactly calls poll(): GET /api/chat/<agent>/messages?since=<cursor>&thread=<id>&read=1 (with limit=50 on the first load).
  5. The panel merges the result with mergeCanonicalMessages() (positive ids de-duplicated and sorted; optimistic negative ids stay at the end), advances the cursor, and marks the conversation read.

The panel also polls on every SSE (re)connect and on a 10 s fallback timer (POLL_MS). A poll error shows "Can’t reach the agent."

Message text is never pushed

An SSE event only says "this conversation moved". The browser then re-fetches from the plugin, which serves every read from its in-memory, write-through copy of the newest historyLimit (default 200) messages per conversation. This keeps the push channel tiny and keeps every read behind the normal permission checks.

The "working…" indicator and the messages endpoint

GET api/messages?thread=&since=|before=&limit=&job=&read=1 returns { messages, hasMore, cursor, readId, pending }.

  • hasMore is computed only when limit truncates the result. cursor is the newest id in the whole conversation, even when a filter applies, so deltas keep working.
  • On Rosie (partyRouting on), a supplier- or customer- conversation is returned as the party sees it: only the party's own messages and LPS's portal-flagged ones (partyVisible). job=<id> narrows it further to messages tagged with that job (a tab's "JR-<id> only" switch).
  • read=1 marks the whole conversation read for the team, not just the fetched page.
  • pending is activeOn(threadId): the plugin fetches GET /api/agents/<agent>/events?status=in_flight&limit=50 (with the caller's replayed auth headers) and answers true only if a non-silent in-flight event has this conversation's threadId. Work on the agent's other conversations, queued events and pending_since do not count. For a supplier's or customer's conversation that is the party's own thread. The panel shows ${agent.name} is working….
  • The panel takes pending only from this server value; it no longer turns it on when you press Send (a message just sent, or still queued, is not work yet).
  • pending_since is still written (set on delivery, cleared by a reply and on harness restart) and still drives the thread event's pending field, but the panel's indicator no longer reads it.

ChatMessage is { id, at, role: 'user'|'agent', text, files: [{name, label, mime, size, dir}], senderName?, senderKind?: 'lps'|'agent'|'supplier'|'customer', portal?, jobRequestId? } (the plugin also returns originThreadId; the portal adds ref).

Paging, panel persistence and destination binding
  • Paging. "Load earlier" fetches ?before=<oldest>&limit=50 (PAGE = 50). It can reach only as far back as the plugin's in-memory historyLimit (200 per conversation). Error: "Can’t load earlier messages."
  • Binding. The panel is bound to a BoundChatDestination {agentId, threadId, revision}. Switching conversation bumps the revision, disables the composer synchronously, discards staged uploads and the draft, and clears the transcript before paint, so a new header can never sit over old messages. Late poll, load or reset results from the previous binding are ignored. This is tested heavily in app/lib/focused-job-chat-routing.test.tsx (rapid JR switches, A→B→A rebinds, upload and voice ownership, reset locking, never-settling requests).
  • Persistence. localStorage['lps.chat.v1'] keeps {open, width, agent, thread} across navigations. sessionStorage['lps.chat.job-page.v1'] records that a focused job page opened the chat, so the next page closes it.
  • Composer. On desktop, Enter sends and Shift+Enter adds a line. In the mobile sheet, Enter adds a line and only the Send button sends. Placeholder Ask <Agent>…; in a supplier's or customer's conversation, Message <party>…; during new-job intake, "Tell Rosie about the new request…".

How database changes reach screens

Chat is only one part of live updates. When anyone writes to the database (a staff member in the admin UI, a supplier in the portal or through a public link, or an agent using its tools), every screen showing that data refreshes itself within a fraction of a second.

One upstream subscription

Browsers cannot subscribe to Supabase themselves: no database key ever reaches them, and app users are not Supabase Auth users. Instead, the app server holds one Realtime channel, lps-workspace, using its existing service client (service_role bypasses RLS for postgres_changes, as it does for queries). The channel is opened once, together with an in-process EventEmitter (no listener limit) that is kept on globalThis['__lps_chat_realtime_v2'], so development hot reloads reuse both instead of opening a new channel.

Watched table(s)Emits
threads (all events)A thread event built from the new row

Broad invalidation is deliberate. DELETE payloads may carry only a primary key, so the relay never tries to work out which job a change belongs to. Every authorised view simply re-reads its own API, with its own access checks.

Refreshing the data views

useJobUpdates(refresh, enabled, eventsUrl = '/api/chat/events') is the hook every live data view uses:

  • Triggers: any jobs event, an SSE (re)connect, window focus, online, the tab becoming visible, and a 60 s fallback interval.
  • Triggers are batched into one refresh() after 200 ms. A change that arrives while a refresh runs marks it dirty, forcing one more read afterwards. Hidden tabs defer the work. Errors are swallowed; each view owns its own error state.
  • Team users: the Jobs page, the Requests page, the customer detail page and the availability-request editor.
  • Portal users go through usePartnerUpdates(supplierId, refresh), which points the same hook at /api/partner/<id>/events: the portal home, closed history, request detail, order detail and orders list.

The Jobs inbox also listens to thread events to patch the chat preview on each jr-<id> card without a full reload (jobInboxEventAction() / patchJobInbox() in app/lib/job-inbox.ts):

Customer conversations are minted on the server

POST /api/customers/:id/chat resolves the saved conversation before opening it and never sends a message:

  1. Check. The id must be a positive safe integer (400 bad id) and the customer active (404 Customer not found).
  2. Reuse. A non-blank rosie_thread_id is returned as is (trimmed).
  3. Mint. Otherwise the server builds customer-<slug>-<id>-<uuid> (slug from the name: lowercased, non-alphanumerics to -, at most 70 characters, default customer) and writes it with a guarded UPDATE that only succeeds if the column is still empty.
  4. Converge. It re-reads and returns the recorded winner, so two people opening the same customer at once end up in one conversation.

The browser must never derive a customer slug itself. On failure the button shows the server's error message, or "Could not open customer chat" when there is none.

Multiple-choice questionnaires

When an agent needs a decision with clear options, it asks with a questionnaire card instead of plain text. You see one question at a time, pick an option (or tick several), or type your own answer in the "Something else" box. A review screen lets you check and edit before you press Submit answers. All answers go back to the agent as one message.

How it works

  1. The agent asks. webchat-cli ask --questions-file q.json [--thread-id] [--portal]. The agent's prompt makes questionnaires the default whenever useful choices exist (including yes/no and "recommended next steps", with the recommended option first and labelled "(Recommended)"), and tells it to wait for the answers.
  2. The plugin validates and stores it. api/reply validates the questions and stores them inside an ordinary message, so no schema change was needed.
  3. The panel renders a card for any agent message that parses as a questionnaire.
  4. You submit. The answers are sent as one normal user message through the same send path (in the portal, through usePartnerChat().send). The agent is woken by that message like any other.

  1. The supplier's thread wakes. api/app-send stores the message (senderKind: "supplier", no origin_thread_id yet) and wakes Rosie on the supplier's own thread.   2. It routes. That thread does nothing but route (message-routing skill 5.0.0, party-thread.md): webchat-cli route --to jr-<id> (or main, up to 5 targets). api/route tags the message with the first target's job and delivers it to every target.

Unread dots and the bell

A conversation is unread when its newest message id is greater than the team's read cursor (threads.web_read_id). Because the cursor lives in the database, reading on one device clears the dot everywhere. ChatNotificationsProvider

Mounted once by the Shell:

  1. Sweep. GET /api/chat/threads on mount, every 60 s and on every SSE (re)connect. This app route reads threads directly: agent in CHAT_AGENTS, last_seq not null, closed_at null, ordered by last_at desc. It returns { threads: { <agent>: [{ threadId, lastId, lastAt, lastRole, lastSender?, lastText, readId }] } }.
  2. Live upserts. Each thread event moves that row to the top; a closed event removes it. A conversation with no messages yet (an id created by an entity trigger) never appears.
  3. Instant clear. markThreadRead(agentId, threadId, lastId) is an in-memory shim (a local map plus a lps:chat-read window event) that clears dots immediately after a poll, before the server's thread event arrives.
  4. Mark all read. markAllRead(items?) calls POST /api/chat/<agent>/read {threadId, lastId} per item. It is the only way to mark a conversation read without opening it.

The provider exposes unread[] ({agentId, threadId, lastId, lastAt, from, lastText}, where from is the sender or the agent's name), unreadByAgent, isUnread() and markAllRead(). The bell popover has agent filter chips: with a chip selected, the action reads Mark read (that agent only); otherwise Mark all read. Each row opens its conversation through openChat().

Ordering, clearing and the canonical queue

Messages to one conversation must reach the agent in the order you sent them, and a "Clear conversation" must not be overtaken by a message sent just before it. The app guarantees this on both sides.

Server: one queue per conversation

Every send and reset runs through enqueueCanonical(JSON.stringify([agentId, threadId]), …) in app/lib/chat-canonical-queue.ts, a first-in-first-out queue per (agent, conversation). JSON tuple keys can't collide, whatever characters the ids contain.

Limit (CANONICAL_QUEUE_LIMITS)Value
perKeyOperations8 queued operations per conversation
activeKeys64 conversations with work queued
totalOperations128 operations overall
  • Capacity is reserved synchronously, before any await, so concurrent requests can't both slip past a limit. Saturation (per-key, active-keys or total-operations) throws CanonicalQueueSaturatedError and the route answers 429 conversation busy; try again.
  • Each queued upstream call has its own AbortController and a 25 s timeout (CHAT_UPSTREAM_TIMEOUT_MS), after which the route answers 504 agent request timed out. The panel's own bound is shorter (20 s), so in practice the browser has usually given up before that 504 is sent.
  • Queued calls deliberately outlive a disconnected browser: once a send is reserved, closing the tab must not undo its place in the order.
  • canonicalQueueSnapshot() gives diagnostics. Tested in app/lib/chat-proxy-ordering.test.ts.

Browser: clear locks

"Clear conversation" lives in the panel's ⋯ "Conversation options" menu, which is hidden on Rosie's supplier and customer conversations (every job and the portal share them). useChat().reset:

  1. takes a lock for that (agent, conversation), so new sends are blocked;
  2. waits for in-flight sends to that conversation to settle;
  3. calls POST /api/chat/<agent>/reset {threadId}, bounded at 20 s;
  4. always reloads afterwards. A failure shows "Couldn’t clear the conversation. Try again.", and the error stays attached to that conversation even if you switch away.
Clearing is destructive

The plugin's api/reset wipes the agent's harness context for that conversation (ctx.resetThread("web", threadId)), deletes all its thread_messages, zeroes both read cursors, pending_since and the preview columns, and resets the in-memory copy so ids restart at 1. The threads row itself stays, because job, supplier and customer foreign keys point at it. There is no undo.

Supplier actions wake Rosie

Suppliers mostly don't chat; they answer availability requests and update orders in the portal. Rosie still needs to know when that happens. So the app rings a doorbell (the supplier-events plugin) that wakes Rosie on the thread of the job the availability request or order belongs to (jr-<id>), because that job thread owns all of the job's supplier work. It comes with a one-line summary such as "<Supplier> submitted their answers on availability request AR-41 (job request JR-12)." The doorbell carries identifiers and, for orders, a short list of what changed (header fields with old and new values, or line statuses and notes). It never carries the full records, so Rosie always re-reads the current data before acting.

Why saves don't wake her

Dropping plain saves was added on 2026-09-29 to cut roughly 52 agent turns a week. Rosie acts on the submission, which carries the final answers. An admin-marked submission produces the same wake-up text as a supplier's; there is no field telling them apart.

App side: notifySupplierEvent()

  • POST ${HARNESS_URL}/webhook/rosie/supplier-events/notify with x-app-secret: <SUPPLIER_EVENTS_APP_SECRET> and a 5 s timeout. Rosie is the only target agent.
  • Best-effort. Without a configured secret the call silently does nothing. A non-2xx answer or network error is logged (notifySupplierEvent: …) and never thrown. The database write has already committed, so a missed doorbell costs freshness, not data. Callers await it, so a slow harness can add up to 5 s to the supplier's request.
  • No retry, no de-duplication. Each accepted call is one wake-up; a re-submit wakes Rosie again.
  • Context builders read the supplier's name and the record's job at event time: supplierAvailabilityEventContext() (app/lib/availability-queries.ts) sets jobRequestId and threadId = jr-<availability_requests.job_request_id> (null without a job), and supplierOrderEventContext() (app/lib/order-queries.ts) sets threadId = jr-<orders.job_request_id>. They no longer read rosie_thread_id.

Harness side

  1. Only POST /notify (else 404); x-app-secret must match (else 401); body must be a JSON object (else 400) with a known type and valid ids per type (else 400). It answers 503 while the plugin has not started.
  2. availability_updated → 200 {ok:true, delivered:false}; nothing is delivered.
  3. Otherwise ctx.notify(type, { text, channelId: thread, threadIdOverride: thread, metadata }) with thread = threadId || "main", then 200 {ok:true}.
  4. Metadata: EventType, SupplierId, SupplierName, then AvailabilityRequestId and JobRequestId, or OrderId with OrderChanges, OrderStatusChanges, or OrderItemId + OrderItemStatus. The event name itself is not shown to the agent, which is why EventType exists.
  • One job, one thread. Every event for an availability request or order with a job lands on that job's jr-<id> thread. Only an event with no job falls back to main, which hands it to the owning job thread with her message-routing skill.
  • A doorbell is not a chat message. It writes no thread_messages row, so it does not appear in the transcript or the bell by itself; only Rosie's resulting webchat-cli replies do.
  • The screens update anyway. The supplier's write also changes watched tables (availability_requests, candidates, orders, …), so a jobs invalidation refreshes team views and portal views independently of the doorbell.
  • Rosie's prompt rules: re-read before acting; ignore deleted or closed records; an event is never approval, customer acceptance, or a pickup/delivery change; never set a selected candidate from an event; an availability submission doesn't confirm an order.

Supplier and customer conversations are never deleted by a job delete. Their messages about the deleted job stay, but lose their job tag: thread_messages.job_request_id is ON DELETE SET NULL, and the plugin clears the same tag in its in-memory copy. Job statuses are described on Status lifecycles.

How the plugin forgets a deleted job
  • The app does not call api/reset on delete; deleteJobRequest just runs the delete_job_request RPC. The webchat plugin reconciles from the tombstones (the 0079 migration comment saying otherwise is superseded by 0097).
  • syncDeletedJobs() (Rosie only) runs at start, before every plugin HTTP request, and every 15 s. It pages deleted_job_threads 1000 rows at a time, adds each id to an in-memory tombstone set and evicts its cache and cursors. It also removes that job's jobRequestId from every cached message in other conversations, matching the database's SET NULL.
  • For a tombstone without purged_at it calls ctx.resetThread("web", id) to wipe the agent's context, then stamps purged_at. If the harness refuses because a turn is in flight, the row is retried on a later pass, including after a restart.
  • Any request for a tombstoned conversation throws "This job chat has been deleted", which the plugin returns as HTTP 410 (relayed unchanged through /api/chat).
  • threads_reject_deleted_jobs (BEFORE INSERT OR UPDATE OF agent, thread_id) refuses to recreate a tombstoned rosie/jr-N row, under an advisory lock.

Known quirks

These are behaviours found by reading the code that a maintainer should know about. None is a data-loss risk.

  • Bell preview of questionnaires. The bell's sweep (GET /api/chat/threads) returns raw last_text, while the SSE relay and the plugin's api/threads apply questionnairePreview(). After a sweep, a bell row for a questionnaire or an answer can briefly show the envelope marker, raw JSON or the Answers to questionnaire <uuid> heading, until the next thread event replaces it.
  • Deep-linked non-canonical conversations can be read but not written (see Conversation id validation).
  • No Range support on api/file despite accept-ranges: bytes (see Files and voice notes).
  • Team-panel attachment labels. The panel sends x-file-name URL-encoded, but the internal api/upload does not decode it before sanitising (only the portal's api/app-upload does). A file named with spaces or non-ASCII characters therefore gets a label with _20-style fragments in the team transcript.
  • api/reply secret check runs only when COGNISPHERE_WEBHOOK_SECRET is set in the harness environment. The harness generates it at boot, so this matters only for an unusual harness launch.
  • Stale documentation. docs/harness/plugin-webchat.md still describes a 2 s poll (the code polls every 10 s as a fallback behind SSE). Comments in chat-notifications.tsx say the bell sweeps each plugin's api/threads and mention a retired read.json; the code reads the database through /api/chat/threads.
  • Reserved-job tags wait for 0148. Until migration 0148 is applied, a message sent in a newly reserved job's chat before Rosie writes the job's row stays untagged (job_request_id NULL, origin_thread_id = 'jr-<id>'), so it does not appear when that job's supplier or customer tab is narrowed with "JR-<id> only".

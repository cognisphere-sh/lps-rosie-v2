Meet the agents

Four AI agents do LPS's routine work. Rosie handles jobs, quotes, supplier requests and orders. Daisy keeps the supplier and plant catalogue current. Lily turns feedback into requirements, and Nova changes the software. You talk to them in the app's chat panel or on Telegram. None of them emails a customer or supplier without approval of the exact draft, places an order without the customer's commitment, or deletes or deactivates anything without the owner's explicit word.

BusinessOperations~15 min read

In short

LPS is a plant-sourcing broker. Customers send plant lists, LPS finds and prices the plants at suppliers, and then it quotes and orders them. Four AI agents do most of the routine work, and Chris, the owner, makes every commercial decision. Each agent has one area of work. They pass anything outside it to the agent that owns it.

Rosie · sales and orders

Turns customer requests into jobs, prices every line from the APS price list, prepares availability requests (ARs) to suppliers, writes quotes and creates orders once the customer commits.

Daisy · supplier intelligence

Finds and onboards suppliers, reads the price and availability lists they email in, loads them into the plant catalogue, keeps contacts and photos tidy, and posts a 9 AM daily summary.

Lily · product and business understanding

Listens to how the work actually goes, records feedback, keeps a written picture of the business and turns repeated pain points into requirements. Reachable on Telegram only.

Nova · platform engineering

Changes the app and the agents' code, keeps the documentation current, and aims to ship a requested app change the same day. A UI change gets a screenshot review first.

Five things are worth knowing from the start:

  • Outside email needs a yes for the exact draft. Rosie and Daisy draft emails and non-routine supplier questions, then wait for approval of that exact draft (from Chris, or for Daisy's supplier email also the developer). A few routine messages have standing permission, such as telling a supplier about an order change. Customers, suppliers and the other agents can never approve for Chris.
  • Orders need a real customer commitment. Rosie creates an order only when the customer has committed. Chris passing on the commitment counts.
  • No deletions without an explicit word. The agents' database tool refuses DELETE statements, so removals are soft: a record is deactivated or closed, not erased. Even that needs Chris's explicit instruction, apart from a few narrow cleanup cases for Daisy.
  • Nothing is invented. An unknown price, name, quantity or date stays blank. The agents don't guess.
  • Every job has its own conversation. Each job has its own chat with Rosie, and that conversation also handles the job's supplier and customer contact. Work and approvals stay with the job they belong to.

The in-app chat panel

On desktop the chat docks on the right of every page. On a phone, open the Agents tab in the bottom bar, choose an agent, then choose a conversation. The panel has an agent selector. Rosie and Daisy also have a conversation picker. Rosie's lists active jobs, suppliers and customers, and Daisy's lists supplier companies. Nova has one general conversation. Lily appears in the list but is greyed out with On Telegram.

  • Attach anything. Use the paperclip, paste an image (a screenshot of a plant list, for example) or record a voice note with the mic. The agent reads or transcribes the file.
  • The agent knows where you are. Each message carries the page you had open, so “price this job” on a job page refers to that job.
  • Quick questions. For a real decision, the agent may send a short multiple-choice form. The suggested option is marked (Recommended), and every question also offers Something else.
  • Action links. Agents post buttons such as Continue in JR-12 chat to move you to another conversation, or Open AR-7 to open an availability request in the side drawer.
  • Progress. While an agent works, the panel shows “Rosie is working…”. Clear conversation in the panel menu wipes a conversation's transcript and the agent's short-term context for it.

Entity chats: a conversation per job, customer and supplier

Each job has its own chat with its own history, which keeps the work separate. Customers and suppliers each have one conversation of their own, which shows what that party sees.

  1. New job. Click New job request (or + on a phone). The app reserves a job number and opens the job page with Rosie's chat for that job, prompting “Tell Rosie about the new request…”. Paste or describe the request and Rosie builds the job.
  2. Existing job. Open a job from All jobs, or use the Rosie button on a job card. On desktop the panel switches to Rosie's chat for that job automatically.
  3. Job tabs. A job's chat has a row of tabs: Rosie first, then the job's customer, then one per supplier (shown by its short name, such as APS, when it has one). The Rosie tab is you and Rosie. Discussion between Chris and Rosie about a supplier or customer belongs there. A customer or supplier tab is that party's whole conversation, with messages from their other jobs tagged JR-<id>. The JR-<id> only switch narrows the tab to this job.
  4. Customer. Use Chat with Rosie on a customer page to open that customer's conversation across all of their jobs. It's created the first time someone opens it. Job cards no longer have a customer chat chip.
  5. Supplier. A supplier row or profile has up to two buttons, Chat with Rosie and Chat with Daisy. They're two different conversations. Rosie's is the supplier's conversation with LPS, and Daisy's covers onboarding, lists and catalogue fixes. A button shows only when that conversation exists.
Watch out

Whatever you type in a customer or supplier conversation (a job's party tab, or Chat with Rosie on their page) goes to that party as LPS. An LPS Partner supplier reads it in their portal. A customer or a supplier without a portal gets nothing: the message is only recorded there, not emailed. Rosie is told silently. To talk to Rosie, use the Rosie tab.

Good to know

The general chat in the panel and the agent's Telegram group are the same conversation. A request typed in one can be answered in the other.

Telegram

Each agent has its own bot and Telegram group, and each bot listens only to its own group. Telegram suits quick requests away from the desk, and it's where Daisy posts the daily summary. Lily is reachable only on Telegram. Nova's group is shared with Chris and the developer, so everyone in it sees her messages. Sending /reset in a group clears that conversation's context.

Email

  • Rosie sends customer and supplier email from Chris's LPS mailbox, as Chris, and only after he approves the exact draft. In the checked-in configuration her inbound polling is switched off, so mail to that mailbox doesn't wake her. Forward it, or paste it into a chat. The mailbox has to be connected under Settings → Rosie → Google account before she can send.
  • Daisy watches a second LPS mailbox and checks it every 5 minutes. Supplier lists, newsletters and account mail sent there reach her automatically. She files each email into the right supplier's conversation and labels it in Gmail.

Suppliers and customers

LPS Partner suppliers chat from their portal with “Local Plant Supply”. The team sees the same conversation in the supplier's tab, and it holds only the supplier's own messages and what LPS (Chris or Rosie) sent them. Internal discussion stays in the job's Rosie tab. When a supplier writes, Rosie passes the message to the job it's about, or to her general conversation if it isn't about a job, and handles it there. Suppliers who only have a public link get no chat. Customers don't use the app or its chat. They correspond by email and messages. Once those are forwarded or routed to Rosie, she handles them in the job's chat. Customers receive only quotes and messages Chris has approved.

Rosie: sales and orders

Rosie is the order coordinator. She runs each job from the first message to the closed order. Chris wants a usable job in minutes rather than an interview, so she works load first, ask later. She saves the job, every line and every price she can find, posts the JR link, and then asks one batch of questions that actually block the next step. Small open points go into the line's internal note (for example “Flat count?”) for Chris to fill in the app.

What she does

  • Intake. Reads a message, screenshot, PDF or plan and creates the job with one line per plant. If no quantity is given she assumes 1 and asks “How many?”. Job names follow Customer or Company | Slug.
  • Pricing. Prices each line from the Austin Plant Supply (APS) price list. LPS buys from APS unless Chris names another supplier. The database creates each line's default candidate (the original request), and Rosie fills in its price. Alternatives are recorded as more candidates. A price she can't find stays blank, and $0 is only ever a real $0 price.
  • Availability requests. A price list doesn't prove stock, so with Chris's OK she sends the supplier an AR for the selected (or original) plant and size on each line. She relays the answers when the supplier submits.
  • Quotes. Writes copy-ready quotes from the saved lines, as a standard quote, a no-totals bid, a ✅/❌ availability list or a partial quote. A quote never names the supplier.
  • Orders. Creates the supplier order once the customer has committed. Each order belongs to one job and one supplier. Its rows start as copies of the ordered lines' selected candidates, and rows can be added by hand later. She keeps the order's additional details (customer, delivery address, contacts, payment) and its change log, follows the supplier's acknowledgement and readiness, tells the supplier about order changes, and closes orders.
  • Messages. Handles customer and supplier messages about jobs and orders, including the supplier-portal chat.

The usual flows

You ask for…What Rosie does
A priceAnswers without creating a job.
A new requestJob and lines → candidates and prices → posts the link and open questions → AR if Chris approves the scope. Quote only if asked.
A quick quoteJob and lines → prices → quote, then stops without an AR.

The stages don't have to run in order. The job moves through Quote → Availability → Committed → Won/Lost as real things happen. An AR delivered moves it to Availability, and creating an order makes it Committed. Only Chris's explicit word closes it as Won or Lost. See Status lifecycles and Quote to order, end to end.

Every write starts with SET app.actor TO 'rosie', so the job_request_changelog triggers attribute her changes. She writes job_requests (with ids from reserve_job_request_id()) and job_request_items. Inserting a line makes the database create and select its default candidate, which she updates rather than inserting her own: unit_price NULL means the price wasn't found, and 0 is a deliberate $0 (migration 0143). Alternatives are plain inserts into candidates. A line's name and size are a plain job_request_items update that never touches a candidate (0144). LPS edits of a line's price, status, available quantity or customer note go through update_job_line, which refuses size and name (0145). She also calls RPCs such as select_candidate, unselect_candidate (back to the default), delete_candidate (a soft delete, refused for a default), record_job_workflow_event (pricing_fetched, quote_shared, availability_sent), create_availability_request, add_candidates_to_availability_request, send_availability_request, answer_availability_request, create_order (job, supplier, line ids and additional details; a job has at most one open order per supplier, 0146), confirm_order, update_partner_order and close_order. create_direct_order no longer exists. On each order she keeps orders.additional_details, an ordered JSON list of field and value pairs (0151), and orders.change_log, a bullet list of material changes only (0150). Customer-facing files go to the storage path lps/rosie_shared/YYYY-MM-DD/. Chris's standing price rules live in knowledge/files/price-rules.md.

App notifications arrive through the supplier-events plugin at POST /webhook/rosie/supplier-events/notify, with event types availability_submitted, order_updated and order_status_saved. The plugin accepts availability_updated but never delivers it. It would also deliver order_status_updated (one order line), but the app doesn't currently send that type. order_updated covers only the order's status (processing, order_ready or completed) and the supplier's own note. The app passes the AR's or order's job thread, so an event lands on jr-<id>, and on main only when there's no job. The plugin doesn't deduplicate or retry: a double submit wakes Rosie twice, and if the harness is down the wake-up is lost (the supplier's save still succeeds). See Harness plugins.

Daisy: supplier intelligence

Daisy is the supplier agent. She keeps LPS's picture of who sells what up to date. Supplier information arrives as emails, spreadsheets, PDFs, photos and websites, and she turns it into clean catalogue data that Rosie and the team can search. Each supplier company has one conversation with Daisy covering all of its locations.

What she does

  • Loads availability. Reads each price or availability list a supplier sends and writes it into the plant inventory, matching each plant to existing rows. An unchanged list is detected and skipped. The newest list wins for what it states, and a plant missing from a list isn't taken as gone.
  • Onboards suppliers. Creates or updates supplier companies, their locations (farms or branches) and contacts, checks for duplicates, and fills in details from the supplier's website.
  • Sorts incoming mail. Works out which supplier each email is from and files it into that supplier's conversation. Job or order mail goes to Rosie.
  • Keeps names consistent. Adds plant name aliases so a search finds the same plant under its other names.
  • Catalogues plant photos. Files, finds, shares and retires plant photos.
  • Answers “who has…?” Searches the inventory and suppliers for a plant or size and suggests substitutes.
  • Sends the 9 AM daily summary. Every morning (Chicago time) she posts a short summary of the previous day to Telegram.
Good to know

The inventory is what a supplier listed, not confirmed stock. That's why Rosie still sends availability requests before an order.

The summary is at most about twelve short lines, numbers first:

Lily: product and business understanding

Lily is the project manager. Her job is to listen. She captures what Chris says about the work and the software, including complaints, shortcuts and half-formed ideas, even when there's no feature request. She keeps a written picture of how the business runs and turns what keeps coming up into clear requirements. She doesn't build anything, write to the database or contact suppliers and customers.

How to reach her

Only on Telegram, in her own group. She has no mailbox and no chat in the app. The app lists her as On Telegram. The other agents and Nova also forward her context, for example when Chris starts designing a feature in Nova's group.

What happens to feedback

  1. Capture. Every piece of feedback is logged straight away in an append-only feedback log, with its date and source (Telegram, Rosie, Daisy or Nova).
  2. Understand. She updates her picture of the business (operations, friction points, photos and sharing, and so on).
  3. Update procedures. If the feedback changes how an agent should work, she passes it to that agent.
  4. Propose. Repeated friction, one costly problem or a clear request becomes a requirement: the problem, the evidence and checkable outcomes, but not the implementation.
  5. Route or close. She sends each part of a requirement to its owner and tracks it until it ships or Chris drops it.

  • “Every Monday I re-check the same three supplier lists by hand.”
  • “What have we decided about plant photos so far?”

What she needs approval for

Chris's explicit feedback is enough for Lily to update the relevant procedure or route a requirement without asking again. If she's only inferring a change, she proposes it and waits for Chris to approve. She sends at most one substantive Telegram message per handoff, decision, planned UI shipment, recurring pattern, closure or blocker, with no receipts or progress pings. Messages to Chris use business language, with no column names or file paths.

Lily's files and handoff format
  • knowledge/files/feedback.md: the raw, append-only log, written by the locked capture-feedback helper. Each entry has a stable source id, date, source (telegram, rosie, daisy or nova), title and text. An identical retry does nothing.
  • knowledge/files/business/: current understanding by topic.
  • knowledge/files/requirements/: one file per change, with an index of status and owner.
  • knowledge/memory.md: durable priorities and directions that were rejected, with the reasons.

Skill: feedback-loop (capture → understand → update procedures → propose → route/close). A handoff to Nova carries the full requirement in the message body: scripts/agent-msg/send --to-agent nova --thread-id main --subject "Requirement: <title>" --message "…". Lily runs with the single thread strategy (everything on main) and has no Supabase access, webchat, Gmail or artifacts, by Chris's instruction. The developer gets technical detail and works on India time.

Nova: platform engineering

Nova is the developer agent. She owns the code of the app and of the agents: screens, plugins, agent instructions, deploy scripts and this documentation. Bug reports, small changes and Lily's approved UI requirements come to her, and the aim is to have a requested change live the same day.

How to reach her

In the app's chat panel (one general conversation) or in her Telegram group, which Chris and the developer share. The other agents send her code requests directly.

How a change ships

  1. Request. A person asks, or Lily sends an approved UI requirement with acceptance criteria. A human saying “deploy”, “push to live” or “do it” counts as authorization.
  2. Build. One builder makes the change as a small, focused edit. Trivial fixes (a word, one style rule) she does directly.
  3. Check. She runs the app's type check and the tests that cover the change.
  4. Visual review (UI changes). She takes preview screenshots at phone and desktop widths from a private preview, never the live app. A separate reviewer marks them PASS or FAIL. A FAIL blocks the release, and a second FAIL goes to a human.
  5. Deploy. She commits and restarts only the app. The agents keep running.
  6. Report. She reports once, in plain language, with a screenshot for UI work. A rollback is one revert and the same restart.

Example requests

  • “What have you shipped recently?”
  • “The Copy order button is cut off on my phone.”
  • “Make the EDD column wider on the job page.”
  • “Rosie's quote template has a typo in the footer.”

When she stops and asks

  • A check, the build or the visual review fails twice.
  • The change touches the database, a business workflow or secrets. Those belong to the developer.
  • The change needs a restart of the agents' server. She runs inside it, so a person has to restart it.
  • The request is ambiguous.

Only a human can add a new approval gate or release step. Nova can propose one but won't build it herself. When Chris starts designing a feature with her, or complains about how the work goes, she points him to Lily and sends Lily the context.

Nova's tools and limits
  • She works in the primary checkout on main and stages only her own paths. She follows CLAUDE.md/AGENT.md and updates docs/harness/ and docs/app/ in the same change. docs/base-harness/ is read-only.
  • She never modifies node_modules/@cognisphere-sh/cognisphere-harness. A harness upgrade goes through the cognisphere-upgrade skill.
  • Check: pnpm --dir app typecheck plus targeted pnpm --dir app exec tsx --test <file>. The full suite isn't run on the host because it needs too much memory. The host has about 8 GB RAM plus 4 GB swap and a Next build peaks around 3 GB, so she runs one heavy step at a time.
  • Deploy: sudo ./scripts/server.sh restart app (builds, then restarts the app unit). She never runs restart harness.
  • Skills: visual-ui-release-qa (preview on 127.0.0.1:3100, captures at 320, 390, 480 and 1440 px, one fresh reviewer thread, PASS/FAIL), create-plugin, create-skill, cognisphere-upgrade.

Approvals: who can say yes

The per-agent tables above say what needs a yes. These rules say how approval works for every agent.

  • Only Chris approves business actions. The developer can also approve Daisy's supplier emails and fixes, and owns schema and tooling changes. Suppliers, customers, templates and the other agents can never approve on Chris's behalf.
  • Approval is for an exact draft. The agent shows the recipient, subject, full text and any link or attachment, and sends exactly what was approved.
  • Approval happens where you asked. If Chris made a request in a job chat and then moved on, the approval question comes back to that job chat. Telegram approvals go through the agent's general conversation. The conversation that owns the work (for a job matter, the job's chat) does the actual send.
  • Silence isn't a yes. A supplier's portal message, an app notification or the absence of a reply never counts as approval. An agent never puts an approval request into a message the supplier can see, or into an email.
  • Standing permissions (no per-case yes needed): Rosie may tell a supplier about an order change, answer routine portal clarifications, send an APS portal AR once its scope is approved, and create the order for a committed request.
  • Feedback changes the rules. Explicit feedback from Chris or the developer (“from now on, always…”) lets an agent update its own instructions straight away. Inferred changes are proposed first.
Good to know

What Chris does in the app counts too. Answers he has already given, and edits he makes in the app (dragging a candidate onto a line, or editing a job or line), are final, and Rosie doesn't ask about them again. From the job page Chris can also publish a draft AR, or drop a candidate onto a supplier's existing AR. Either one reaches the supplier straight away, unless the AR is still a draft.

Each agent works only in its own area and passes everything else on with an internal message. You don't need to know who owns what. Ask whichever agent is in front of you, and the request reaches the right one with its context. Internal handoffs never reach customers or suppliers.

How agent messaging works

The core agent-messaging plugin carries handoffs. An agent runs scripts/agent-msg/send --to-agent <agent> --thread-id <thread> --message "…" [--subject "…"] [--silent], and the receiver gets an agent_message event with From, FromThread and Subject metadata. The sender's identity comes from the harness-set $PI_AGENT_ID/$PI_THREAD_ID, so it can't be spoofed. Sending to a thread id that doesn't exist yet starts a fresh thread, which is how task threads (<thread>-§-[TASK]-§-<slug>) are spawned. By default (allowMessageFrom unset) any agent may message any other, and this deployment's docs record no restriction. Failures: 403 means the sender isn't allowed, 404 means an unknown agent or the plugin isn't running, and “could not reach … inbox” means the harness is down.

Rosie's handoff payload states the path (quote_only, ordinary availability work, committed intake, or accepted quote with skip_availability: true), the commitment evidence, known ids, and OriginThreadId/OriginChannel unchanged. For a send it also includes the recipient, channel, full text and approval evidence. Daisy's dispatch still sends job-mail pointers to Rosie with --to-agent rosie --thread-id router. Rosie's current skills have no rule for a router thread (the legacy-forwarding rule went in message-routing 4.0.0).

Each agent's roster of teammates lives in system_prompts/0.1-agent-directory.md, which is regenerated only when missing. Some rosters still carry older teammate descriptions (for example, Rosie described as never contacting suppliers). The deployment prompts quoted on this page are authoritative.

Guardrails built into every agent

RuleWhat it means in practice
Never invent dataAn unknown price, name, size, quantity, date or contact stays blank. A price that wasn't found is left blank. A price of 0 means a real $0 price.
Never name the supplier to a customerQuotes and customer messages never name or hint at the supplier.
Notes by audienceNote for customer holds only what the customer should see. Internal note holds the customer's requirements and open points, and never reaches customer output. Supplier answers live on candidates.
Suppliers see only what's meant for themIn the portal chat, only replies marked for the supplier are visible. Internal notes and approval requests never are.
No deletionsThe agents' database tool refuses DELETE, DROP, ALTER and TRUNCATE statements and any table, index or view changes. Removals are soft (a deactivation or a close) and need Chris's word.
Every change is traceableRosie's edits to jobs and lines appear in the job's change history with her name.
Shared pages are private by defaultPages that Rosie, Daisy or Nova publish (for example a report) are visible only to signed-in LPS users, unless an agent deliberately makes one public for content that's fine for anyone. A signed-in reader can also switch a page between public and private.
The SQL guard and storage rules

scripts/agent/supabase/query (Rosie and Daisy) wraps psql and allows only SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE, WITH, EXPLAIN, transaction control, SET, SHOW, VALUES and CREATE [OR REPLACE] FUNCTION|PROCEDURE. Everything else is refused, including DELETE, DROP, TRUNCATE, ALTER, GRANT, COPY and DO. The guard doesn't scan the bodies of functions an agent creates, so it isn't a complete barrier, and the prompts' no-delete rule still applies. Deletions go through RPCs such as delete_candidate or is_active flags. The lps storage bucket is public, so nothing confidential goes there. Plant photos use the private plant-photos bucket. Artifacts are published private by default (<app>/private/artifacts/<slug>, signed-in users only), and public only with --public.
